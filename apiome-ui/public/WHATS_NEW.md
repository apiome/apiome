# Apiome 11-2026 RC10

We continue to improve the platform based on your feedback with improvements and new features!

---

## Features/Improvements

- **Contracts: every command has a screen** — each of the 28 `apiome pact` commands can now be done from **Contracts**: publishing consumer pacts and provider contracts, running a verification, managing pacticipants, versions and environments, settings, migrating, exporting and guided set-up, each screen showing the same action as an `apiome pact` command, a Pact CLI command, a GitHub Action step and a REST request. The landing's **Publish provider contract**, **Run a verification** and **Derive a pact from a project** now open their screens instead of a command dialog. **Pact Broker overview** in the docs lists every screen under **The Contracts workspace**
- **Guided set-up for contracts** — **Contracts** → **⋯** → **Set up contracts** takes a workspace from nothing to a yes or no before every deploy on one page you can leave and come back to. Six sections, each **Done**, **Next** or **To do**: Apiome proposes pacticipants from your projects and repositories; you tick environments; it creates a CI key scoped to publishing pacts, reading verdicts and recording deployments, writes `PACT_BROKER_BASE_URL` and `PACT_BROKER_TOKEN` into your GitHub repositories as Actions secrets, and opens a pull request adding can-i-deploy and record-deployment; you choose how the first pact comes to be (publish your Pact tests' files, or derive one from the spec) and turn on the spec as the provider contract. Without a linked GitHub account the secrets and the workflow are given to copy instead. A workspace migrated from a broker arrives with the first two sections done. From a script: `apiome pact setup --from-projects --environments production,staging --ci github`.
- **Provider test scaffolding** — a fourth card on the **Export** page, and `apiome pact scaffold-provider-tests`, writes a ready provider verification test from a provider's pacts in TypeScript (pact-js), Java (pact-jvm), Go (pact-go) or Python (pact-python): wired to this broker through `PACT_BROKER_BASE_URL` / `PACT_BROKER_TOKEN` (no secret in the file), the recommended selectors with pending pacts on, and a handler stub for every provider state with the parameters it is sent with. States with no handler in Apiome are flagged in the states table and in the file; **Download** or **Copy** it
- **Export a contract** — the new **Export** page takes a contract out of Apiome three ways. **A pact from a project** derives a consumer's declared surface as a Pact v4 file, with an operation checklist saying where each example came from (the spec's own, or generated from the schema — review it) and why an operation was left out; untick operations before you **Download pact**, or **Review and publish** it on the Publish page. **A Postman collection** replays a stored pact (latest, on a branch, with a tag, a version or a SHA), every consumer of a provider at once (one folder each) or an uploaded pact file, with a `{{baseUrl}}` variable, provider-state setup as pre-request scripts and a bar of how many interactions the collection keeps — what it leaves out is listed with the reason. **A pact file** downloads any published pact exactly as stored, or every pact for a provider as a zip. A pact's page, a pacticipant and Export studio link here, and each kind shows its `apiome pact export-consumer-contract` / `export-postman` command; the CLI gains `--all-consumers`, `--state-setup` and `--state-change-url`
- **Migration switch-over** — the Migration wizard's **Switch over** step now proves the move: Apiome asks your old broker and itself the same can-i-deploy questions and reads *Copied. Apiome and acme.pactflow.io give the same answers.* — or *38 of 40 agree.* with each difference and why (a result that was not copied, a selector the old broker does not support). **Watch who has moved** lists where each service last published, Apiome or the old broker. Switch on **Keep copying until everyone has moved** and every 15 minutes what is new at the old broker is brought over — the token is kept encrypted only while it is on — until you switch it off, or it stops itself after the old broker has been unreachable for 24 hours. `apiome pact migrate --follow` does the same from a terminal
- **Migration wizard** — **Contracts** → **Migrate from a broker** moves a Pact Broker or PactFlow into your workspace in four steps. **Connect** with the broker's URL and a token (used for this migration only, never stored) and **Test connection**. **Choose** what to copy from a dry run's counts — by kind, how much history, and whether Apiome's or the source's record wins a conflict — with everything not portable in one **Before you copy** strip. **Copy** runs on Apiome's servers with a progress bar, a row per kind, every failure with its reason and a live log; you can leave the page, **Stop** it, and **Resume** without copying anything twice. **Switch over** shows the two variables that point your pipelines at Apiome and downloads the report. **Past migrations** reopens any earlier run
- **Contracts settings: secrets, commit statuses, CI recipes and agents** — **Contracts** → **Settings** gains four tabs. **Secrets** lists every workspace secret with what uses it (write-only: a value is never shown again), with **New secret**, **Replace value** and **Delete**, which is blocked while anything uses the secret; a secret a migration could not copy reads **No value**, and its webhook stays off until you **Set value**. **CI and commit statuses** switches statuses on the consumer's and the provider's commit, and bi-directional verdicts can now set statuses and fire webhooks too; below, a working pipeline for GitHub Actions, GitLab CI, Jenkins and Azure Pipelines and the broker address. **Verification schedule** lists what Apiome verifies on its own. **Agents** says what MCP-connected agents may do with contracts: reading is on, recording deployments is off until you allow it, and never to production
- **Contracts clean-up rules, preview and restore** — **Contracts** → **Settings** opens on **Clean-up**: say what to keep as sentences — every deployed or released version (always), the latest versions of each main branch, the latest of every other branch for so many days, anything with a tag — each with how many versions it keeps. **If it ran now** shows how many versions, pacts and results the rules would remove, redrawn as you edit, with **See the list**. Turn on **Run clean-up nightly**, choose how long deleted items stay restorable, and **Restore** anything from **Recently deleted** until it is purged. Without the Contracts admin permission the tab is read-only. `apiome pact retention show`, `set` and `preview` do the same from a pipeline
- **What is running where** — **Contracts** → **Environments** opens on a **Board**: each service down the side, each environment across (production last) and **Released to users**, every running version with a dot for whether it is verified with everything beside it, and — where the environment to its left runs a newer version — whether that version **can go** or is **blocked** (the deploy simulator's answer). **History** lists every deployment, undeployment, release and end of support with who recorded it and whether it was verified at the time. An environment's menu has **Record an undeployment**, a release has **End support**, and unticking a release in **Record a release** ends its support. `apiome pact record-undeployment` and `record-support-ended` do the same from a pipeline
- **Environments: create, record a deployment, record a release** — **Contracts** → **Environments** now has **Record a deployment**, **New environment** and **Record a release**, and each environment a menu to edit or delete it. Recording a deployment asks can-i-deploy first and shows the reasons for a “no”; recording anyway needs an explicit tick, which is written to the audit trail. Recording a release lists the versions that stay supported, and an environment cannot be deleted while versions run there. `apiome pact create-environment`, `record-deployment` and `record-release` do the same from a pipeline
- **Version management and badges** — a pacticipant's page has a **Versions** tab: search its versions, narrow them to the main branch, **Deployed**, **Unverified** or **Deleted**, add a tag inline with **+ tag**, delete a version (with an optional reason) and **Restore** it from **Deleted** with its pacts, results and deployments until it is purged; select several to tag, delete or restore them together. A version that is deployed or released cannot be deleted — record its undeployment first. Its **Settings** tab has **Badges**: preview the README badge exactly as it is served and copy its Markdown (Apiome SVG or shields.io). `apiome pact create-version-tag`, `delete-version` and `restore-version` do the same from a pipeline
- **Create, edit and delete a pacticipant** — a pacticipant's page has a **Settings** tab for its name, display name, main branch, repository, owner, linked project and labels, and the Pacticipants page has **New pacticipant**. Renaming keeps the handle and the history, and the old name keeps working (a pact published under it still lands on the same pacticipant). **Delete** says how many versions, pacts and results go and which providers lose a consumer, asks you to type the handle, and can be undone for 30 days — from the dialog or **Show → Deleted** on the list; a pacticipant with a version deployed cannot be deleted. `apiome pact create-or-update-pacticipant` and `delete-pacticipant` do the same from a pipeline
- **Live verification runs and results upload** — **Run verification** now opens a run drawer that follows the run: each consumer's progress, the failing interaction with expected beside actual, and the log. **Re-run this interaction** checks one fix without publishing, **Reproduce on the mock** opens the provider's project, and **Stop run** ends the run before its next interaction; closing the drawer keeps it going and a toast says when it ends. **Upload results** attaches a report from a verifier you ran yourself — Pact verifier JSON, JUnit, Schemathesis, Newman or Dredd, detected from the file — and a report Apiome cannot read is refused with the reason
- **Run a verification** — **Contracts** → **Verification** is the screen for `apiome pact verify`: choose the provider and the version under test, then let Apiome run it against a target (or **Add a target…**) or copy the Pact verifier command for a pipeline Apiome cannot reach. Choose the pacts as sentences with a count each (**Recommended**, **Everything** or **Custom**), with pending and work-in-progress pacts, and set provider states up with Apiome's handlers, the provider's own setup endpoint or not at all. A preview lists what will run and what is skipped as already verified for this version (**Run anyway**). Requests that change data are never sent to a production target, a target that never answers ends the run as *Could not connect* rather than a failure, and **Save as a schedule** keeps the provider verified on a cadence
- **Publish a provider contract automatically** — on **Contracts** → **Publish a provider contract**, tick **Do this on every publish** on **A project version** and each later published version of the project becomes the provider's contract (drafts never do), or use the new **Repository** tab to follow an OpenAPI or AsyncAPI file of a GitHub repository: every push to its branch publishes it, with the commit as the provider version. **Published automatically** under the provider says where its contract already comes from, with any warning (no push webhook, no project bound), and stops a source for new publishes. Every automatic publish is compared with each consumer's pact like any other — the verdicts the **If you publish now** preview shows — and recorded in the access audit with its outcome
- **Publish a provider contract** — **Contracts** → **Publish a provider contract** is the screen for `apiome pact publish-provider-contract`: take the contract from a project version, an uploaded OpenAPI or AsyncAPI document or a URL, say whose version it is, and choose the evidence that the provider behaves that way — run Apiome's hosted verifier now, attach a verifier's report, reuse a recorded contract-test run, the pipeline's exit code, or none yet — each labelled with its strength. Before anything is saved, **If you publish now** shows each consumer's verdict (with the first difference and where that consumer is deployed) and **Nobody uses** lists the operations no pact touches; **Compare only** publishes nothing. A failed run publishes nothing and lists the failing operations, an unverified contract is marked and never unblocks a deploy, and the result dialog links to can-i-deploy
- **Write a pact by hand** — **Publish a pact** has a new **Write interactions** tab for teams with no Pact tests yet: list your interactions on the left and fill each one in on the right — description, provider state with parameters (suggested from the provider's state catalogue and its mock's scenarios), the request and the response, with the body fields the consumer relies on as matcher chips. HTTP, message, GraphQL and gRPC interactions sit in one list. Each path is checked against the provider's spec and flagged, not blocked, when it is missing; **Fill from the spec** takes the response's status, example and matchers. The editor writes a Pact v4 document that is checked and published like any other, and **Edit in Write interactions** opens a pasted pact in it.
- **Check a pact against several provider versions before publishing** — on **Publish a pact**, **Check it before it counts** compares each pact with the provider's main branch, what is deployed to production, any other environment, the latest draft of the provider's project or any version you type, all at once and without saving anything. The aside shows one pill per provider version with how many interactions match, and marks each interaction **Match**, **Differs** or **Not in spec**; **See the difference** opens what the pact needs beside what the spec declares, with a link to the spec's versions. Provider versions that could not be found are listed with the reason, and the same check is shown as an `apiome pact check` command
- **Publish a consumer pact** — **Contracts** → **Publish a pact** now opens a full page: drop several pact files or a whole `pacts/` folder (each row shows its consumer → provider, Pact specification and interaction count), paste one, or derive one from a project's declared consumer. Give the consumer version, branch, tags and build URL, fill the branch from the linked repository, tag with the branch name, and **Check only** to see whether each pact is new, unchanged or a conflicting pact for that version before anything is written. A file that is not a pact is marked with the reason and never blocks the others; the result says which pacts are new and pending, which were unchanged and what was not sent. The page shows the same action as an `apiome pact publish` command, and the **Import Pact file** dialog links to it pre-filled
- **The same, from anywhere** — the Pact webhook editor now shows the webhook you are building as an `apiome pact create-webhook` command, a `pact-broker` command, a GitHub Action step and the REST request, updated as you type and ready to copy. Passwords, credential headers, tokens and signing secrets appear only as `$WEBHOOK_…` variables, and anything the command cannot carry is listed with the reason. Behind it, a CI check keeps every `apiome pact` command and every Contracts screen that changes something in step
- **A landing page for Contracts** — **Contracts** in the rail now opens on an overview instead of the Pacticipants list: one status strip for failing, stale, provider-less and pending integrations, a facts line whose **Show stats** reveals the contract-health rollup, and every consumer → provider integration in one table with its kind (Pact or bi-directional), latest verdict, the environments both sides run in and when it was last verified — searchable, filterable by **Failing**, **Stale**, **Bi-directional** or **Mine**, and drawable as a network. **Start something** groups the ways in by consumer, provider and pipeline, with a first-run checklist until one integration is verified. Every Contracts page now has the same tab row (**Overview**, **Pacticipants**, **Matrix**, **Can I deploy**, **Environments**, **Webhooks**, **Health**, **Settings**) in place of the buttons on the Pacticipants header
- **Pacts in your specs, gates and registry** — the Pact Broker now feeds the rest of Apiome: a published pact becomes the consumer's next contract revision, the deploy gate has a `contracts` signal with the can-i-deploy verdict, a version publish can warn or be blocked on failing contracts (the **Contracts** card in **Publish**), `apiome diff --consumers` names the pacticipant behind each consumer, and public project pages in Browse and the portal show pact badges and consumers. **Pact Broker overview** in the docs ties it together under **Inside the registry**
- **Publishing a pact updates the consumer's contract** — a new pact published to the Pact Broker API is now declared as the consumer's next contract revision in the provider's project, so breaking-change analysis sees consumers that publish pacts; interactions that cannot be placed are kept with a reason, and a provider not yet bound to a project is reported in the publish response
- **Block a publish on failing contracts** — the publish dialog gains a Contracts card, and a policy of off, warn or block decides what a failing Pact verdict does to a publish. A blocked publish can be forced with a reason, which is recorded; a verification that is still missing shows as pending and never blocks.
- **can-i-deploy in the deploy gate** — the project deploy gate now includes a `contracts` signal: the same can-i-deploy verdict the Pact Broker API gives, for the pacticipant bound to the project. A failing verdict fails the gate; a verification result that is still missing is reported as unknown and left out of the verdict.
- **Self-host Apiome as your Pact Broker** — `docker-compose.pact-broker.yml` runs a standalone broker (Postgres, the REST API and automatic HTTPS) answering the Pact CLI, the Pact libraries and `can-i-deploy`, with sizing guidance, encrypted scheduled backups and a step-by-step upgrade with rollback
- **Move from Pact Broker or PactFlow** — `apiome pact migrate --from <your broker>` imports a Pact Broker or PactFlow workspace: pacticipants and labels, versions with branches and tags, pacts, verification results, environments, current deployments and releases, and webhooks, each with its original date. Run it with `--dry-run` first to see what would be imported and what cannot be carried over (PactFlow secrets are listed for re-entry); running it again changes nothing and resumes where it stopped
- **A faster Pact Verification page and matrix** — with 100,000 versions in a workspace the **Verification** page now loads its status in about 11 ms instead of over half a second, and every matrix and `can-i-deploy` answer stays under 30 ms
- **Pacticipant owners** — a pacticipant's page has a new **Owner** section: choose a team and click **Assign owner**. The team is told when a verification of one of the pacticipant's pacts fails and when a stale-pact re-publish request opens or expires. Without an owner, notices go to the owner of the consumer or project it is bound to, else to the **Unowned findings** report
- **Secrets for webhooks and verification targets** — keep a CI token, API key or signing secret once as a workspace secret (REST `/v1/tenants/{tenant}/pact-secrets`) and refer to it from contract webhooks as `${user.<name>}` — in a header, the URL, the body, the password, a GitHub token or the signing secret — and from verification targets with `auth.kind` `secret`. The value is sealed, never shown or logged, and put in only when a webhook is sent or a run starts; rotating the secret updates everything that uses it, and a secret in use cannot be deleted
- **Contract health and verification analytics** — on **Pacticipants**, **Contract health** rolls every consumer ↔ provider pair into one state — **Healthy**, **Degraded**, **Failing** or **Unknown** — from its latest verdict, pass rate, flakiness (how often the verdict flips), whether it is overdue, an open re-publish request and failed webhook deliveries, worst first, over the **Last 7**, **30** or **90 days**. Open a pair to read each signal and why, and to see its pass rate, flakiness and mean duration charted per day. The Verification page shows each consumer's health next to its name
- **Stale pacts and verification scheduling** — on **Pacticipants**, **Verification settings** sets when Apiome verifies pacts on its own (**On publish**, **Hourly**, **Nightly** or **Manual only**), how long a pact may go unverified before it is stale, and per-provider or per-pair overrides with the verification target scheduled runs use. A stale pact is marked **Overdue** on the Verification page and its consumer is asked to re-publish it: **Re-publish requests** tracks each request through **Acknowledged**, **Re-published**, **Cleared** or **Expired**, notifies the consumer's owners and escalates what expires
- **Pact Broker: permissions, audit and isolation** — every Pact Broker change, from the Pact CLI, a Pact library, the console or an MCP tool, is now checked against the **Pacts** permission and recorded in **Access audit** as a `pacts.*` event with who acted and what changed (never the request body). The database also refuses any Pact row that points at another workspace's data
- **Publish pacts from the import dialog** — on a project's **Consumers** screen, **Import Pact file** now has **Also publish to the Pact Broker**: name the **Provider** and **Consumer version** (and optionally a **Branch**, **Tags** and **Build URL**) and the pact is stored as a consumer contract and published, so the provider can verify it and can-i-deploy sees it. A refused publish is shown beside the stored revision
- **RC10 begins** — RC9 is complete, and its notes are kept in the **Apiome RC9** release notes. The version on the launcher, in the user menu and on **Help & docs** now names the new release candidate (`RC10`). RC10 is about **contracts**: Pact Broker and PactFlow compatibility, bi-directional contract testing, and contract agreements
- **The MCP Catalog opens faster on large catalogs** — the **Servers** grid, its filters and **Analytics** now read each server's tool counts, safety flags and complexity from what was stored at discovery instead of working them out on every load. In a workspace with 2,000 servers of 50 tools each, the grid went from about 8 seconds to under 50 ms, and a filtered view from about 7.5 seconds to under 10 ms. **Analytics** numbers refresh every 30 seconds.
- **Faster search suggestions on the browse site** — the search palette (**⌘K** or **/**) on the public browse site now answers each keystroke in under 50 ms; on a large catalog it took up to a second. It suggests by **name** — organizations, API products, projects, operations, schemas and properties — and shows `40+` when a kind has more matches than it read. **Press ↵ on the first row** to run the full search. The counts in the browse site's top bar and the lists on its home page now refresh every 2 minutes, so a newly published version can take that long to be counted there; its own page and search show it at once.
- **Long lists load a page at a time** — **Export history**, the **MCP capability directory**, **Published versions**, a connector's **Items** and **Refresh runs**, and the admin **Users**, **Tenants**, **Licenses** and **Templates** lists now read one page and offer previous and next page arrows or **Load more**, so a workspace with thousands of rows opens as quickly as a small one. A count that would mean reading every row shows as `10,000+`
- **Pact Broker: pacticipants, versions, branches and tags** — the REST API now keeps the registry Pact tooling names applications by: register a **pacticipant**, record each build as a **version** with its branch and tags, and read every branch's head. A version never changes once recorded, a branch head only moves forward, and deleting a project keeps its pacticipant. **Roles** has a new **Pacts** row for it: **Editor** can view, create and edit; only **Owner** and **Admin** can delete. Verification results and can-i-deploy build on this and are not yet available
- **Pact Broker: publish and fetch pacts** — the Pact CLI and Pact libraries now publish to Apiome. Give `pact-broker publish` or pact-js `publishPacts` your workspace's broker base URL (`…/v1/tenants/<workspace>/pact-broker`) and an API key as the token. Consumers and providers are registered the first time a pact names them, a build's pacts are stored all together or not at all, and identical pacts are stored once, so ten builds of an unchanged pact give the provider one pact to verify. A different pact for a version that already has one is refused; an invalid pact is refused with the place in the file. Verification results and can-i-deploy are not yet available
- **Pact Broker: choose pacts to verify with selectors** — a provider's Pact verifier now fetches the pacts it must verify from Apiome. Send consumer-version selectors such as `{ mainBranch: true }`, `{ matchingBranch: true }` or `{ tag: "prod", latest: true }` with the same broker base URL and token you publish with. Each pact comes back once, with the reasons it was chosen in the words the Pact Broker uses, and an ambiguous selector is refused with the place in the request. You can also read the latest pact of a consumer from a tag or a branch. Environment selectors select nothing until deployments can be recorded
- **Pact Broker: publish verification results** — a provider's Pact verifier now reports back to Apiome. Run it with `publishVerificationResult: true`, a provider version and a branch, and each pact it verified gets a result: passed or failed, with the outcome of every interaction kept as the verifier sent it, the build URL and the provider branch. Results never change once published and are numbered per pact, and a provider version is registered on first use. A result can link an Apiome provider verification report instead of copying it. Can-i-deploy will read these results; it is not yet available
- **Pact Broker: environments and deployments** — your deploy jobs can now tell Apiome what runs where. Create environments such as `test` and `production` with `pact-broker create-environment`, then run `pact-broker record-deployment` after each deployment: it replaces the version deployed there before and keeps the old one in the history. Several copies of an application in one environment are recorded per **application instance**, and apps with several live versions use `record-release` and `record-support-ended`. A provider verifier's `deployed`, `released` and `environment` selectors now return the pacts of what is recorded there.
- **Pact Broker: can I deploy?** — `pact-broker can-i-deploy --pacticipant "Billing Service" --version "$GIT_COMMIT" --to-environment production` now asks Apiome whether a version is compatible with what runs in an environment, and stops your deploy job when it is not. The answer is **yes**, **no** or **unknown**, with the reason for each integration in the Pact Broker's words: a consumer needs its providers to be there, a provider does not need its consumers, and every version live in the environment is checked. `--retry-while-unknown` waits for a result that has not arrived yet, `--ignore` leaves one application out, and an environment marked `contractRequired` refuses a version with no pact. The same answer is on `GET …/pact-broker/can-i-deploy` and the matrix on `GET …/pact-broker/matrix`
- **Pact Broker: pending and work-in-progress pacts** — a consumer's new expectation no longer breaks the provider's build the moment it is published. With `enablePending: true` in the provider's verifier, a pact the provider branch has not verified yet comes back **pending**: its failure is reported but does not fail the build, and the first successful result from that branch ends the pending state. A branch you create later inherits what was already verified, so a new feature branch does not start with every pact pending. Add `includeWipPactsSince` and the verifier also gets **work-in-progress** pacts: the latest pact of each consumer branch that your selectors did not choose and the provider has not verified, always as pending. The matrix shows whether each result was for a pending or work-in-progress pact
- **Pact Broker: compatibility tested in CI** — the official `pact-broker` CLI, pact-js and pact-jvm now run against Apiome on every Pact Broker change, and **Pact Broker compatibility** in the docs lists every Pact Broker endpoint as supported, partial, planned or not planned. Three differences the tests found now match the Pact Broker: the first version published on `main` (or `develop`, `master`) sets the main branch, results from the Ruby `pact-provider-verifier` are accepted, and the pact-js verifier can fetch its pacts. An endpoint Apiome does not serve answers `501` with a link to the page and the issue that tracks it, never a bare `404`.
- **Pact Broker: contract webhooks** — Apiome can now tell your CI when a pact needs verifying and when a verification finishes. `pact-broker create-webhook` and `create-or-update-webhook` work unchanged with the broker base URL and token you already use. Use the `contract_requiring_verification_published` event to start a provider build: it fires once for each provider version that has not verified the pact yet — the head of the main branch and what is deployed or released — and carries that version and its branch. `${pactbroker.pactUrl}` and the other Pact Broker parameters are filled into the URL, headers and body. A webhook can start a **GitHub Actions** workflow directly, sign each request with a secret of yours, and is retried three times before it is marked failed; the log of every attempt can be read back. Tokens and passwords are sealed and never shown again. There is no screen for webhooks yet
- **Pact Broker: labels, deletes and retention** — group pacticipants with labels (`team-billing`, `tier-1`) and list the ones carrying a label. Deleting a version or a pacticipant now hides it, with its pacts, results and deployments, and you can restore it for 30 days; a deployed version is only deleted when you force it. A retention run keeps the newest versions of each branch, anything deployed and anything recent, and deletes the rest — it always shows you the plan first, and applies only that plan. Every delete and cleanup is in the audit log
- **Pact Broker: one overview page** — the Pact Broker core is complete, and **Pact Broker overview** in the docs walks through it end to end: the consumer publishes, a webhook starts the provider, the verifier reports back, deploy jobs record what runs where, and `can-i-deploy` decides. It links the page for each step, and **Help & docs** finds it when you search for `can-i-deploy` or `contract testing`. There is no screen for pacts yet
- **Pact Broker: publish a provider contract** — a provider build can now register its OpenAPI document against its version, the first step of bi-directional contract testing. `PUT …/pact-broker/contracts/provider/<provider>/version/<version>` with the document (OpenAPI 3.0, 3.1 or 3.2, in JSON or YAML), or with `apiomeVersion: "ledger-api@1.4.0"` to use an Apiome version's document; a provider bound to its project can say just `latest`. The branch, tags, build URL and self-verification results are recorded with it, the same document is stored once however it was sent, and a different contract for a version that already has one is refused. Swagger 2.0 and anything else that is not OpenAPI 3.x is refused with the place in the document. Comparing the contract with consumers' pacts is not yet available
- **Pact Broker: comparison rules** — how a consumer's pact is compared with a provider's OpenAPI document is now fixed and published. **Comparison rules** in the docs lists every check, every failure with its code, and how each Pact matching rule is read. A result is `pass`, `fail` or `partial`: `partial` means nothing contradicts the pact, but something (a `date` matcher, a non-JSON body) could not be compared in full. The comparison is not run for you yet; the verdict will appear in the matrix and can-i-deploy in a later release
- **Pact Broker: bi-directional verdicts in can-i-deploy** — once a provider publishes its OpenAPI document as a provider contract, Apiome compares every consumer pact that needs it with that document, as soon as either side publishes, and stores the outcome as a verification result. `pact-broker can-i-deploy` and the matrix read it exactly like a result from a Pact verifier: a failing comparison stops the deploy and says so, and each matrix row's `verificationResult.kind` reads `native` or `bidirectional`. A new contract on a branch is compared again with the pacts the branch's previous contract was compared with, each pair is compared once, and the publish answer lists every comparison it ran
- **Pact Broker: self-verification evidence for provider contracts** — a provider contract is now trusted only when evidence says the provider matches it. Publish it with the report of the tool that tested the provider against its own document — Schemathesis (JUnit or NDJSON), Dredd, Newman (JSON or JUnit) or any JUnit writer — or cite an Apiome provider-verification report, and Apiome reads it and records the kind, tool, SHA-256 digest and counts in `selfVerification`: `verified`, `failed`, `inconclusive` or `missing`. Without passing evidence the contract's comparisons read `unverified`, can-i-deploy warns (or, if your operator sets the `fail` policy, says no), and each matrix row says `providerContractVerified`. The new `apiome pact publish-provider-contract --evidence <report>` command sends the contract and its report in one step
- **Pact Broker: comparison fidelity report** — a green bi-directional verdict now says how much it is worth. The new **Pact comparisons** page (`/ade/dashboard/pacts/comparisons`) lists the workspace's recent comparisons, each with its verdict *and* its strength — only **Compared in full** is green — and opens each one's report: every check of every interaction marked **Compared**, **Partly compared**, **Failed** or **Not run** (with the reason), the matching rules the comparison could not prove, and what it could not compare, each with its place in the pact. The same report is on `GET …/pact-comparisons/<resultId>/fidelity`, linked from each bi-directional result as `apiome:comparison-fidelity`
- **Pact Broker: export a declared consumer surface as a pact** — a consumer you registered on **Consumers** by picking operations and fields can now get a Pact v4 file without writing contract tests. `apiome pact export-consumer-contract --project <project> --consumer <consumer>` writes one interaction per declared operation and response status, with only the declared fields and the specification's own examples as sample values, and `apiome pact publish-consumer-contract … --consumer-app-version <version>` publishes it to the Pact Broker so the consumer appears in the matrix, can-i-deploy and bi-directional comparisons. Every value it had to invent and everything it had to leave out is listed with a reason. Importing a Pact v4 file now also reads each body correctly
- **Pact Broker: compile a pact into a contract suite** — the REST API can now turn a published pact into replayable verification cases, the first step of hosted provider verification. `POST …/v1/tenants/<workspace>/pact-suites/provider/<provider>/consumer/<consumer>/pact-version/<sha>` gives one case per HTTP interaction with the request the consumer recorded, the response it expects, its provider states and its matching rules. The same pact version always gives the same suite and the same `sha256:` digest, whichever build published it. Message and plugin interactions, and anything else that cannot be replayed, are listed with a reason.
- **Pact Broker: bi-directional contract testing is complete** — a provider can now be checked against its consumers without running their pacts: publish its OpenAPI document with `apiome pact publish-provider-contract --evidence <report>`, and Apiome compares every consumer pact with it, trusts the result only when the provider's own test report passed, and feeds the verdict to the matrix and `can-i-deploy`. A new **Bi-directional contract testing** post in the release notes ties the pieces together, and **Pact Broker overview** in the docs now shows where it fits next to Pact verification
- **Pact Broker: verify a provider from Apiome** — a provider no longer needs a Pact library to be verified. `POST …/v1/tenants/<workspace>/pact-verifier/pacts/provider/<provider>/verify` selects the pacts the provider must honour with the same consumer-version selectors a Pact verifier sends, replays every interaction against a registered verification target, and publishes a verification result per pact, so the matrix and can-i-deploy answer from it. Responses are compared under the pact's matching rules, and each mismatch names the interaction, the field, the rule that failed and both values. Mutating interactions are sent only when the target's policy allows them and the run opts in; a run can never widen the target's policy. Interactions that assume a provider state are skipped unless you say the target already holds the data, and a run that skipped anything publishes nothing rather than a pass it did not earn. See **Verify a provider from Apiome** in the docs
- **Pact Broker: provider states** — a provider can now register the states its pacts name ("an invoice 1042 exists") and how the hosted verifier sets each one up: a call to a state-change URL, or a recorded fixture of requests sent to the verification target, with a different setup per target when needed. Each interaction's states are set up before it is replayed and torn down after. **Provider states** (`/ade/dashboard/pacts/provider-states`) lists a provider's registered states and checks its pacts against them before a run: a state that is not registered, a required parameter an interaction leaves out, or a setup the target's policy forbids is flagged, and that interaction is held back rather than guessed at. See **Provider states** in the docs
- **Pact Broker: every Pact matching rule in hosted verification** — the hosted verifier now applies every matching rule of Pact specifications 2, 3 and 4, and is tested against the whole Pact specification test suite. A `contentType` rule judges an image, a PDF or another binary response by its bytes, so any JPEG matches `image/jpeg`. Three results now agree with other Pact verifiers: a `regex` must match the whole value, a pact that records an empty body accepts only an empty response, and an `Accept` header ignores the case of its parameters. Generators are ignored, as every verifier ignores them, and a rule that cannot be applied still fails with its name rather than passing. **Supported matching rules** in the docs lists each one
- **Pact Broker: send a verifier report from your own CI** — a verification that already ran in your pipeline now counts. `apiome pact publish-verification-results report.json …` (or `POST …/pact-version/<sha>/external-verification-results`) takes a `pact_verifier_cli --json` report or a JUnit file and stores it as a verification result, so the matrix and can-i-deploy see it. Nothing is dropped silently: every field Apiome could not use comes back as a note with its location, a skipped test case is never counted as verified, and a report that proves nothing is refused instead of published as a pass. See **Send a report from your own CI** in the docs
- **Pact Broker: message pacts against AsyncAPI** — a provider that sends messages can now be checked against its consumers without running either. Publish its AsyncAPI 2.x or 3.x document as its provider contract (`contractType: asyncapi`, or leave it out), and every consumer's message pact — Pact 3 `messages` or Pact 4 asynchronous messages — is compared with it as soon as either side publishes, with the verdict in the matrix and can-i-deploy. Each message is matched to a message the provider sends, by the topic in its metadata and its name, then its contents, headers and content type are checked. An Avro payload, a synchronous message or an HTTP interaction is listed as not compared, never passed silently. See **Message pacts** in the docs
- **Pact Broker: gRPC and GraphQL pacts in hosted verification** — a gRPC call recorded by the Pact protobuf plugin is now replayed against a `grpc` or `connect` verification target, and its reply's status and message are checked under the pact's matching rules, naming the field that drifted. GraphQL operations recorded as HTTP interactions are replayed as GraphQL: a `query` is no longer held back as a mutating `POST`, and a reply with `errors` the consumer did not record fails. A streaming method, a subscription or another plugin is listed as unsupported with the reason, and a pact that mixes HTTP and gRPC is `incomplete` on one target, never green. See **gRPC and GraphQL pacts** in the docs
- **Pact Broker: the provider verification engine is complete** — Apiome can now verify a provider against its consumers' pacts by itself, with no Pact library in the provider. It compiles each pact, sets up the provider states the pact names, replays every interaction against a registered target, judges the replies by every Pact matching rule, and publishes a result for each pact to the matrix and `can-i-deploy`. Message pacts are checked against the provider's AsyncAPI document, and results from your own CI can be sent in. A new **Provider verification engine** post in the release notes ties the pieces together, and **Pact Broker overview** in the docs now shows where hosted verification fits
- **Pact Broker: the `apiome pact` commands** — the Apiome CLI now has the `pact-broker` commands your pipeline already runs: `apiome pact publish`, `can-i-deploy`, `record-deployment`, `record-release`, `create-environment`, `create-webhook`, `list-pacticipants` and `describe-version` take the same flags, print the same lines and exit the same way (`0` when it worked, `1` otherwise), and read `PACT_BROKER_BASE_URL` and `PACT_BROKER_TOKEN`. `apiome pact verify` runs hosted verification in one step, and `--output markdown` writes a table ready for a job summary. A file that is not a pact or a flag Apiome cannot honour stops the command with the reason before anything is sent. `apiome pact migrate` is listed but not available yet. See **The apiome pact commands** and **Run Pact in CI** in the docs
- **Pact Broker: basic auth and Pact key scopes** — Pact tooling set up for basic auth now works unchanged: send your workspace API key as the password (`--broker-username` can be anything), or as the token as before. **API keys** has three new presets so a pipeline never needs full access: **Pact: read-only** (`pact:read`, for `can-i-deploy`), **Pact: consumer + deploy** (`pact:read` `pact:publish`, to publish pacts and record deployments) and **Pact: CI verifier** (`pact:read` `pact:verify`, to publish verification results — it cannot publish a pact). Each reaches the Pact Broker and nothing else, and the key's member still needs the **Pacts** permission. `apiome pact` reads `PACT_BROKER_PASSWORD` as the key when no token is set. See **Pact keys and scopes** in the docs
- **Pact in CI: a GitHub Action and GitLab and Bitbucket pipelines** — the three Pact steps of a pipeline are now ready-made. On GitHub, `apiome/pact-action` publishes the consumer's pacts, asks `can-i-deploy` and keeps its answer in **one pull-request comment** that updates on every push, and records the deployment after a merge; it refuses to record a deployment from a pull request. GitLab CI and Bitbucket Pipelines get the same three jobs as copy-and-paste pipelines. A missing broker URL, token, pacticipant or environment stops the step with the reason. See **Run Pact in CI** in the docs
- **Pact in CI: Jenkins and Azure Pipelines** — copy-and-paste pipelines for the three Pact steps. The Jenkinsfile reads the broker key from a Jenkins credential; the Azure pipeline maps its secret variable into each step and stops with the reason when it is missing. Both record a deployment on the main branch only, never from a pull request, and keep the `can-i-deploy` answer with the build. See **Run Pact in CI** in the docs
- **Pact verdicts on GitHub and GitLab commits** — when a consumer publishes a pact, its commit now shows **pending** until the provider verifies it. Then both the consumer's and the provider's commits show **success** or **failure**, as the status `apiome-pact/<consumer>/<provider>` that a branch protection rule can require. Register the repositories under **Repositories** with a linked account and set each pacticipant's repository URL; publish with the commit SHA as the version. A status that can't be sent is kept with the reason and can be retried. See **Commit statuses** in the docs
- **Pact Broker: pact tools for AI agents** — an agent connected to Apiome's MCP server can now work with your pacts. `pact.list` lists the latest pact of each consumer and provider, with how its last verification went. `pact.matrix` and `pact.can_i_deploy` answer **yes**, **no** or **unknown** with the reasons, like `apiome pact can-i-deploy`. `pact.record_deployment` records what was deployed where. The tools use an MCP key and the **Pacts** permissions of the person who created it. Answers are kept short: lists come a page at a time, and the tool says how many rows it left out. An unknown argument is refused with the reason. They form a new **pact** toolset in **MCP settings**
- **Pact Broker: export a pact as a Postman collection** — `apiome pact export-postman --provider "Ledger API" --consumer "Billing Service"` writes a consumer's pact as a Postman Collection v2.1, to open in Postman or run in CI with `newman run`. Each interaction becomes one request to `{{baseUrl}}`, with its provider states in the description, a saved example of the expected response and a test script that checks the status, headers and body. Pick a pact by consumer version, tag, branch or pact version, or export a local file with `--pact-file`. Message and gRPC interactions, values under a matching rule and generators are listed with the reason, never dropped silently
- **Pact Broker: CLI, CI and client compatibility is complete** — a pipeline that runs `pact-broker` moves to Apiome by changing the command to `apiome pact`: same flags, same log lines, same exit codes, and `PACT_BROKER_BASE_URL` / `PACT_BROKER_TOKEN` (or basic auth with the key as the password) work as they are. Give each job a key with a **Pact** preset, drop in the GitHub Action or the GitLab, Bitbucket, Jenkins or Azure Pipelines recipe, and see each pact's verdict on the commit. AI agents get the `pact.*` MCP tools, and any pact exports as a Postman collection. A new **CLI, CI and client compatibility** post in the release notes ties the pieces together, and **Pact Broker overview** in the docs now lists CI pipelines and AI agents as places to work from
- **Contracts: the Pacticipants list and a pacticipant's page** — the rail has a new **Contracts** entry under **Ship**. It opens **Pacticipants** (`/ade/dashboard/pacts/pacticipants`): every application in your workspace's pacts, with its handle, main branch, labels, repository and last update, narrowed by **Label** and read 50 at a time with **Load more**. Click a name to open its page: an **Overview** of the handle, main branch, repository, labels and bindings; its **Branches** with their head versions, the main branch marked **Main**; what each **Environment** runs of it now (versions **Deployed now**, with their application instance, and releases **Released and supported**); and its **Versions**, latest first, with their branches, tags, builds and who recorded them. Click a branch or a tag to list only its versions, and **Clear** to list them all again. An empty workspace shows the `apiome pact publish` command that registers its first pacticipants. Needs **Pacts** → **View**. See **Pacticipants** in the docs
- **Contracts: read a pact** — a pacticipant's page has a new **Pacts** section: every pact it is in, as the consumer or the provider, with the latest consumer version and how its last verification went. Click one to open the pact: its **Overview** (consumer and provider, consumer version, pact version, specification, interactions), its **Verification status** (**Verified**, **Failed**, **Failed (pending)** or **Not verified yet**, and every provider version that verified it), the **Changes since the previous pact** (interactions added, changed — with each changed value, old and new — and removed), every **Interaction** with its provider states, request and response or message, and the **History** of consumer versions with their verdicts.
