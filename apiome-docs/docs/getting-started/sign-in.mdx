Apiome opens on the sign-in page. Single sign-on comes first: each identity provider your
administrator has enabled has its own button, and the email form sits underneath.

<Screenshot id="login" alt="The sign-in page: Welcome back, with Email Address and Password fields and a Sign In button" />

## Sign in

1. **Open Apiome** in your browser. On the local stack that is the apiome-ui address you started
   (see [Run Apiome locally](./run-locally.md)).
2. **Choose your provider** — for example **“Continue with GitHub”** — and approve the request on
   the provider's page. You come back to Apiome signed in.
3. Or sign in with email: if the form is folded away, **click “or use your email”**. Fill in
   **Email Address** and **Password**, then **click “Sign In”**.

   On the local stack, use `ada@example.com` with the password `apiome-dev`.

A wrong password shows **“Incorrect email or password.”** under the field — check both and try
again.

## Sign in with your company's single sign-on

If your workspace uses its own identity provider (Okta, Microsoft Entra ID, Google Workspace and
others, over SAML or OIDC), the sign-in page shows **“Sign in with SSO”**.

<Screenshot id="login-sso" alt="The sign-in page with Sign in with SSO open: a Work email field holding ada@northwind.io and a Continue with SSO button" />

1. **Click “Sign in with SSO”.**
2. Enter your work email in **Work email** — for example `ada@northwind.io` — and **click
   “Continue with SSO”**.
3. Sign in on your company's page. You come back to Apiome signed in.

The first time you sign in this way, Apiome asks you to finish setting up your account, as it does
for any new sign-in. If you already have an Apiome account with the same email, single sign-on is
added to it.

**“No single sign-on is set up for this email domain.”** means your domain has no identity
provider connected — sign in another way, or ask your administrator. If your company's provider
refuses or Apiome cannot verify its answer, you land back here with a message saying so; see
[Single sign-on](../admin/single-sign-on.mdx#when-sign-in-fails) for what each one means.

## Pass the two-factor check

If your account has two-factor authentication, Apiome asks for a code before it lets you in —
after your password, and after you sign in with a provider such as GitHub or Google or through
single sign-on.

1. With an authenticator app, enter the six-digit code from the app in **Authentication code**.
2. With email, **click “Send email code”**, then enter the code from your inbox in **Email code**.
   The **Authenticator** and **Email code** tabs switch between the two when both are set up.
3. **Click “Verify and continue”.**

**“Back to sign in”** returns to the first page without signing you in.

### Skip this step on a device you trust

Tick **Trust this device for 30 days** before you verify. Apiome then stops asking for a code when
you sign in from this browser, for 30 days. Leave it unticked on a shared computer. To undo it,
**click “Forget this device”** on your [profile](../workspace/profile.mdx#once-it-is-on).

### Sign in without your device

If you have lost your phone or cannot reach your authenticator, use one of the backup codes you
saved when you turned two-factor on.

1. **Click “Lost your device? Use a backup code”** under the form.
2. Enter a code in **Backup code** — ten letters and digits, like `ab12c-de34f`. Capitals matter.
3. **Click “Verify and continue”.**

Each backup code works once. After you are in,
[make a new set](../workspace/profile.mdx#make-new-backup-codes) so the old ones stop working.
**“Use a verification code instead”** returns to the code form.

If you have no backup codes left, ask an owner of your workspace or an administrator of your Apiome
installation to reset your two-factor. You are signed out everywhere and sign in again without a
code; Apiome then shows a notice — *Your two-factor authentication was reset* — with
**“Set up two-factor”**, which takes you to your profile to turn it on again.

### Too many wrong codes

After 10 wrong codes in a row — from the authenticator, email or a backup code — two-factor
sign-in is locked for 15 minutes and the page says *Two-factor sign-in is locked for 15 minutes.*
A correct code is refused too until the lock ends. Wait, then **click “Back to sign in”** and sign
in again. A correct code resets the count.

Each sign-in also allows only a few attempts. When the page says *Too many incorrect codes for
this sign-in* or *This sign-in step has expired*, **click “Back to sign in”** and start again.

## Create an account

Don't have an account yet? **Click “Create one”** under the sign-in form. You can sign up with a
provider or with an email address and a password; see [Create an account](./create-an-account.mdx).
