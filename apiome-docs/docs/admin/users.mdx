**Users** holds every account on the installation, across all workspaces. **Sign in to the
[admin console](./admin-console.mdx) and choose Users** in the sidebar.

This is the installation-wide account list. To manage who belongs to *one* workspace and with which
role, use [Members & seats](../workspace/members.mdx) in the app, or [Tenants](./admin-tenants.mdx) here.

<Screenshot id="admin-users" alt="Users: three cards, then five accounts with license, verified and enabled badges, creation date and last active time, one of them Never" />

The three cards at the top are live:

| Card | Shows | Underneath |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Total Users** | Every account | How many were created in the last 7 days |
| **Verified Users** | Accounts with a verified email | How many are enabled |
| **New Users** | Accounts created in the last 30 days | *Last 30 days* |

**Refresh** reloads the cards and the list. The result of every action shows in a banner above the
cards for five seconds.

People create their own accounts on the [sign-up page](../getting-started/create-an-account.mdx),
under the installation's [sign-up policy](./auth-providers.mdx#sign-up-policy). There is no queue of requests to
approve.

## Find and manage an account

The list shows every account with its **License**, **Status**, **Created** date and **Last active**
time. **Status** shows two badges: **Verified** or **Unverified**, and **Enabled** or **Disabled**.

**Last active** is the later of two times: the account's last completed sign-in, by any method, and
the last time one of its sessions was refreshed while in use. A sign-in that is still waiting for its
second factor doesn't count. An account no one has signed in to reads **Never**.
Hover over a **Created** date to see the time.

**Click the ⋮ button** on a row for its actions:

<Screenshot id="admin-users-menu" alt="A user's actions: Mark Unverified, Disable User, the license list, Manage Feature Flags and Delete User" />

| Action | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| **Mark Verified** / **Mark Unverified** | Sets whether the email counts as verified. An unverified account cannot sign in. |
| **Disable User** / **Enable User** | A disabled account cannot sign in. Nothing is deleted. |
| **Assign License…** | Opens the list of enabled licenses; **click one** to assign it, replacing any license the user had. **Remove License** appears when the user has one. See [Licenses](./licenses.mdx). |
| **Manage Feature Flags…** | Opens **Feature Flags** for this user — the flags their license grants, and per-user overrides. **Click “Done”** to close it. See [Feature flags](./feature-flags.mdx). |
| **Reset two-factor…** | Shown for a user who has two-factor on. Clears it so they can sign in again — see [Reset a user's two-factor](#reset-a-users-two-factor). |
| **Delete User** | Deletes the account — see below. |

## Create an account

To add someone who has not signed up:

1. **Click “New User”** at the top right.
2. Fill in **Name**, **Email** and **Password** (at least 8 characters; it is stored as a bcrypt
   hash). Give the person this temporary password by a channel you trust.
3. Leave **Mark email as verified** and **Enable account immediately** ticked so they can sign in
   at once, or untick either to hold the account back.
4. **Click “Create User”.**

<Screenshot id="admin-users-create-dialog" alt="Create New User with a name, email and temporary password, both boxes ticked" />

The email is stored in lower case, and the new account appears in the list.

## Reset a user's two-factor

Someone who has lost their authenticator **and** their backup codes cannot get past the two-factor
step. Resetting their two-factor lets them sign in again with their password or provider alone.

Confirm who you are dealing with before you do this — a reset is exactly what an impostor would ask
for.

1. **Click the ⋮ button** on the user's row, then **“Reset two-factor…”**. The
   action appears only for users who have two-factor on.
2. Enter a **Reason** of at least 10 characters. It is kept in the audit trail; the user does not
   see it.
3. **Type the user's email address** in the confirmation field. **Reset two-factor** stays off until
   it matches.
4. **Click “Reset two-factor”.**

<Screenshot id="admin-users-reset-two-factor" alt="Reset two-factor for Priya Raman: a reason entered and her email typed to confirm" />

The reset:

- removes the user's authenticator and backup codes, and turns two-factor off for the account;
- forgets every device they marked as trusted;
- signs them out everywhere;
- is recorded in the authentication ledger with the user, the reason and that the super-admin did
  it.

The dialog then says whether the user was emailed. If it says **no email was sent**, your
installation has no email sender configured — tell the user yourself. The next time they sign in
they are not asked for a code, and Apiome shows them a notice to
[set two-factor up again](../workspace/profile.mdx#turn-it-on).

A workspace owner can do the same for a member who belongs only to their workspace — see
[Reset a member's two-factor](../workspace/members.mdx#reset-a-members-two-factor).

## Delete an account

1. **Click the ⋮ button** on the row, then **“Delete User”**.
2. **Type the user's email** in the confirmation field. **Delete user** stays off until it matches.
3. **Click “Delete user”.**

<Screenshot id="admin-users-delete-dialog" alt="The delete confirmation: the user's email typed to confirm, and the Delete user button" />

The account, every tenant membership it carried and any license assigned to it are gone; this cannot
be undone. To stop someone signing in but keep their account, **disable** it instead.

## Sign-up requests from earlier versions

Earlier versions had a **Pending Signups** tab: people asked for an account and a super-admin
approved them. That queue is retired — the tab, the request form and its actions are gone.

Anyone still waiting in it gets one email, *Finish creating your account*, the first time the
upgraded app starts. It links to the sign-up page, where they enter the same email address and
choose a new password; the password from their old request is never used. The email is sent only
when:

- [email sign-up](./auth-providers.mdx#email-sign-up) is configured — if it is not, configure it
  and restart apiome-ui to send the emails then;
- the address has no account yet — an address that already has one is skipped;
- the [sign-up policy](./auth-providers.mdx#sign-up-policy) allows the address — while sign-up is
  closed, by invitation, or limited to other domains, the request waits, and a later restart sends
  it once the policy allows it.

Each address is emailed once, even with several app servers. The old requests stay in the database,
read-only, for one more release.

## With the API

There is no REST endpoint or CLI command for these screens: apiome-ui reads and writes the
`apiome.users` table through its own server. Inside one workspace, membership and
roles are managed from [Members & seats](../workspace/members.mdx) instead.
