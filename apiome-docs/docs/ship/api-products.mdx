An **API product** is the thing a consumer is given access to — "Payments API" — rather than the
projects behind it. One product bundles several **members**: projects at a version or at latest, MCP
servers and catalog items, under one name, with a **state**, a **visibility** and an owner team.
**Go to Ship → Products.**

<Screenshot id="api-products-list" alt="Products: one row per product with its handle, summary, state, visibility and member count, and the New product button" />

Each row shows the product's name, its handle and summary, its **State** (**Draft**, **Published**,
**Deprecated** or **Retired**), its **Visibility** and how many **Members** it has. Click a name to
open the product.

## Create a product

1. On **Products**, **click “New product”**.
2. Enter a **Name**, the way a consumer would ask for it — for example *Payments*.
3. Optionally enter a **Handle** (`payments`). It is used in URLs and plans and is derived from the
   name when left empty. It cannot be changed later.
4. Optionally add a **Summary** — *Card payments, refunds and the payments MCP server.*
5. **Click “Create product”.** The product opens, as a **Draft** with **Private** visibility and no
   members.

## Add members

<Screenshot id="api-product-editor" alt="A product: the details form with visibility and state, and the members table with each member's version, environment and status" />

Below the details, the add-member row takes one member at a time:

1. Choose a **Kind**:

   | Kind | What it includes |
   | --- | --- |
   | **Project at latest** | A project at whatever version it published last. The product follows every new publish. |
   | **Project at a version** | A project at an exact version (`1.4.0`) or a range (`2.x`, `2.1.x`): the newest published version in range. |
   | **MCP server** | An MCP server from the catalog. |
   | **Catalog item** | A spec in the catalog that is not a project. |

2. Name the member by its **slug or id** — for example `payments-api`.
3. For **Project at a version**, enter the **Version** — for example `2.x`.
4. Optionally enter an **Environment** (`production`, `sandbox`) to narrow the member to one; leave it
   empty for every environment.
5. **Click “Add member”.**

The **Members** table shows, for each member, its **Kind**, its **Version** as what it was added with
and what it means today (`latest → 1.4.0`, `2.x → 2.3.1`, or `nothing yet` when no version is in
range), its **Environment** and its **Status**: **Published**, **Not published**, or **Deleted** when
the project or server behind it is gone. **Click “Remove”** on a row to take a member out; the
project or server itself is untouched.

A product never stores a version number for **Project at latest** or a range: it reads the newest
published version every time, so publishing `payments` 1.5.0 moves the product to 1.5.0 with no edit.

## Visibility and state

Change both in **Details** and **click “Save product”**. Only the fields you changed are saved.

| Visibility | Who sees the product |
| --- | --- |
| **Private** | The owner team and workspace admins. |
| **Workspace** | Everyone in the workspace. |
| **Portal** | Readers of the developer portal. |

**Portal** needs every member published. While one is not, the option is unavailable and the hint
under **Visibility** names the members to publish first — *Portal needs every member published. Not
published: Payments MCP.* The same rule applies when you add an unpublished member to a product that
is already on the portal: the add is refused.

**State** is the product's lifecycle — **Draft** while you assemble it, **Published** once consumers
can ask for it, then **Deprecated** and **Retired** — and is shown on the list.

## Owner team

A product has one owner team. The **Owner team** picker appears in **Details** when Teams is available
in your workspace; otherwise set it with the REST API (`owner_team` on
[the products endpoints](../reference/rest/api-products.md)).

## Plans

A **plan** is a tier of the product — *Sandbox*, *Standard*, *Partner* — and what it promises a
consumer: how they authenticate, how many requests they may send, who approves a request and which
gateway environments enforce it. Apiome records the plan; the gateway enforces it.

<Screenshot id="api-plan-editor" alt="The Plans table with Declared only and auth-mismatch flags, and the plan editor open on Standard with its declared-only notice, auth warning and Plan fields" />

The **Plans** table, under **Members**, shows each plan's **Auth**, its **Limits** (`100 / minute ·
50,000 / day`), its **Approval**, its **Environments** and its **State**. **Declared only** marks a plan
Apiome cannot provision yet, and **1 member does not match** marks an auth mismatch (see below).

### Add a plan

1. **Click “New plan”.** The plan editor opens under the table.
2. Under **Plan**, enter a **Name** — for example *Standard*. Optionally enter a **Handle**
   (`standard`); it is derived from the name when left empty and cannot be changed later. Add a
   **Description** and pick a **State**.
3. Under **Access**, choose the **Auth strategy**:

   | Auth strategy | What a consumer uses | Settings |
   | --- | --- | --- |
   | **API key** | A key in a header | **Key header** — for example `X-API-Key` |
   | **OAuth2 client credentials** | A client id and secret from your identity provider | **Identity provider config** and **Scopes** (`payments:read refunds:write`) |
   | **Mutual TLS** | A client certificate | — |
   | **None** | Nothing | — |

   Then choose the **Approval** — **Manual** (a person approves each subscription request) or
   **Automatic** — and optionally a **Maximum key age (days)**, for example `90`.
4. Under **Limits**, enter how many **Requests** a consumer may send **Per** window: second, minute,
   hour, day or month. **Click “Add limit”** for another window — for example `100` per minute and
   `50000` per day. Each window takes one limit, and a count must be a whole number of at least 1.
   Optionally set **Concurrency**, the requests in flight at once.
5. Under **Environments**, tick the gateway environments the plan is provisioned to — for example
   **Kong · prod-cp**. They are the environments your gateway connectors report under **Bring in → Connectors** (see
   [gateway configs](../reference/rest/gateway-configs.md)).
6. Under **Terms**, optionally enter a **Terms URL** (`https://example.com/terms`), a **Pricing
   note** shown to consumers (*$49 a month*) and a **Billing reference** for your billing system. The
   billing reference is internal: it is never exported or shown on the portal.
7. **Click “Save plan”.** The plan joins the table and stays open in the editor with its notices.

To change a plan, **click “Edit”** on its row, change the fields and **click “Save plan”**. To remove
one, **click “Delete”** and confirm with **“Delete plan”**; its handle becomes free again.

### Declared only

Apiome provisions a plan onto a gateway through that gateway's connector. Until a connector can
provision, a plan whose environments belong to it is saved as **Declared only**, with a notice in the
editor — *Declared only: kong-prod-cp cannot be provisioned yet (its connector has no provisioning),
so Apiome records the plan but changes nothing on the gateway.* A plan with no environment is declared
only too. The plan, its limits and its terms are still recorded, exported and shown to consumers.

### Auth must match the members

A plan's auth strategy should match a security scheme that every member declares: an **API key** plan
needs an `apiKey` scheme, **OAuth2 client credentials** an `oauth2` or `openIdConnect` scheme, and
**Mutual TLS** a `mutualTLS` scheme. Each member whose current version declares none is listed in a
warning in the editor — *Refunds API declares no API key scheme, so this plan's auth does not match
it.* The plan is still saved; add the scheme to the member's spec, or choose another strategy. MCP
servers are not checked.

### Plans on export

When you export a version that a product includes, an OpenAPI export carries the product's plans as
the `x-apiome-plans` extension. Other formats have no equivalent, so the plans are dropped and the
fidelity report says so — see [Understand export fidelity](./export-fidelity.md#api-plans).

## Products in the dependency graph

Each product is a node of the dependency graph with an **Includes** edge to every member. When you
ask what a breaking change in a project reaches, the blast radius lists the products that include it,
with their owner team — so you know which products, and through them which consumers, a change
touches. Deleting a product takes its edges out of the graph.

## Delete a product

At the bottom of the product, **click “Delete product”**, then **“Delete product”** again to confirm.
The projects and MCP servers it bundled are untouched, and its handle becomes free again.

## Permissions and the API

Products use the **Projects** permissions: **view** to read, **create** to create, **edit** to change
details, members or plans and **delete** to delete. Everything on this page is also available from
[the REST API](../reference/rest/api-products.md):
`GET | POST /v1/tenants/{t}/products`, `GET | PATCH | DELETE /v1/tenants/{t}/products/{slug}`,
`GET | POST /v1/tenants/{t}/products/{slug}/members` with `DELETE …/members/{id}`, and
`GET | POST /v1/tenants/{t}/products/{slug}/plans` with `GET | PATCH | DELETE …/plans/{plan}`. A plan
read returns `declared_only`, its `notices` (`api-plan-declared-only`) and its `warnings`
(`api-plan-auth-mismatch`).
