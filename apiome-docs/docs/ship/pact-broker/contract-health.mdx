**Contract health** rolls every consumer ↔ provider pair with a pact into one state, built from what
the Pact Broker already records about it: verification results, staleness, re-publish requests and
webhook deliveries. It also shows how each pair's verification has trended: its **pass rate**, its
**flakiness** (how often the verdict flips) and its **mean duration**, per day or per week.

Health is worked out each time you open the page. Nothing is stored, and nothing needs turning on.

## Before you start

- You hold **Pacts** → **View**. Without it, the page shows **Permission denied**.
- A pair appears once its consumer [publishes a pact](./publish-pacts.mdx). Its trends fill in as
  [verification results](./verification-results.mdx) are published.

## Open Contract health

1. In the rail, under **Ship**, click **Contracts**.
2. Click the **Health** tab.

<Screenshot id="pact-health" alt="The Contract health page: a strip of counts (3 pairs, 1 healthy, 1 degraded, 1 failing, 87% pass rate, 17% flakiness), then the pairs worst first: Orders UI → Ledger API failing on its latest verdict, pass rate and flakiness, Billing Service → Ledger API degraded because it is overdue with an open re-publish request, and Orders UI → Inventory API healthy."/>

The strip at the top counts the pairs in each state, and gives the pass rate, flakiness and mean
duration of every listed pair together.

The **Pairs** table lists the pairs worst first: **Failing**, then **Degraded**, **Unknown** and
**Healthy**, then by provider and consumer.

| Column | What it shows |
| --- | --- |
| **Pair** | The consumer and provider. Click it to open the pair's page. |
| **Health** | The pair's state (see below). |
| **Concerns** | Each signal that fails or warns, failing first. Hover one to read why. |
| **Runs** | Verification results published in the window. |
| **Pass rate** | The share of those results that passed. |
| **Flakiness** | How often the verdict changed from one result to the next. |
| **Mean duration** | The mean run time of the results that carry one. |
| **Last verified** | When the newest pact's newest result was published. |

Use the two lists above the table to narrow it:

- **Window**: **Last 7 days**, **Last 30 days** (the default) or **Last 90 days**.
- **State**: one state, or **Every state**.

Both are kept in the page address, so you can share the view. The counts still cover every pair. The
page lists at most 500 pairs; if more match, it says how many are left out, so narrow it by state.

## How a pair's health is worked out

Each pair has six **signals**. Each one is **OK**, **Warning**, **Failing** or **No evidence**:

| Signal | OK | Warning | Failing | No evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Latest verdict** | The newest pact's newest result passed. | The newest pact has no result yet. | The newest pact's newest result failed. | — |
| **Pass rate** | 95% or more of the window's results passed. | 80% to 95%. | Under 80%. | No result in the window. |
| **Flakiness** | The verdict changed between 10% or fewer of consecutive results. | Over 10%, up to 30%. | Over 30%. | Fewer than three results in the window. |
| **Overdue** | The newest pact was verified within its [stale threshold](./stale-pacts.mdx). | It was not. | — | The stale-pact sweep has not checked the newest pact yet. |
| **Re-publish request** | No request is open. | A [re-publish request](./stale-pacts.mdx#re-publish-requests) to the consumer is open. | — | — |
| **Webhooks** | The newest delivery of each [contract webhook](./webhooks-console.mdx) that fired for the pair did not fail. | At least one newest delivery failed for good. | — | No webhook fired for the pair in the window. |

The pair's state is the worst of its signals:

| State | When |
| --- | --- |
| **Failing** | At least one signal is failing. |
| **Degraded** | At least one signal warns, and none fails. |
| **Healthy** | Every signal with evidence is OK. |
| **Unknown** | No signal has evidence yet. |

A signal with **No evidence** never makes a pair worse. It is still listed on the pair's page, so you
can see what was not checked.

:::note Flakiness counts flips, not failures
Results *passed, failed, passed, failed* have a pass rate of 50% and a flakiness of 100%: the verdict
changed every time. Results *passed, passed, failed, failed* have the same pass rate but a flakiness
of 33%: one change in three. A pair that fails steadily is failing; a pair that flips is flaky, which
usually means a test that depends on timing, data or order.
:::

## Open a pair

Click a pair. Its page shows:

<Screenshot id="pact-pair-health" alt="The health of Orders UI → Ledger API over the last 7 days: Failing; the newest pact 4.2.0; 14 runs, 5 failed, 64% pass rate, 6 verdict flips, 46% flakiness, 2.1 s mean duration; and the six signals with their reasons: latest verdict, pass rate and flakiness failing, webhooks warning."/>

- **Health**: the pair's state, its newest pact, when that was published and last verified, and the
  window's runs, failures, pass rate, verdict flips, flakiness and mean duration. If a re-publish
  request is open, **Open re-publish request** opens it.
- **Signals**: each of the six signals with its status, why, and its reason code (for example
  `pact-health-pass-rate-low`).
- **Trends**: a chart each for **Pass rate**, **Flakiness** and **Mean duration**, one point per day
  (per week for **Last 90 days**). A day with no result has no point, so the line breaks there
  rather than dropping to zero. Hover a point to read its day and value.
- **Trend**: the same numbers as a table, newest first, with days that had no result included.

<Screenshot id="pact-pair-health-trends" alt="The Trends of Orders UI → Ledger API over the last 7 days: pass-rate, flakiness and mean-duration charts with a gap on 2026-11-23, when nothing was verified, and the Trend table with one row per day from 2026-11-26 back to 2026-11-20."/>

Results published by a verifier outside Apiome often carry no duration. They count towards the pass
rate and flakiness, but not the mean duration, and the page says how many were left out.

Change the **Window** list to see another window. In the header's **⋯** menu, **Open the pact** opens
the pact, and **Verification history** opens the provider's [Verification page](./verification-console.mdx) narrowed to this consumer.

## On the Verification page

The provider's [Verification page](./verification-console.mdx) shows each consumer's health state
next to its name. Click it to open the pair's health.

## Who can do what

| To | You need |
| --- | --- |
| Open Contract health and a pair's health | **Pacts** → **View** |

Every read is scoped to your current workspace: another workspace's pairs never appear, and naming
another workspace's pacticipant answers *not found*.

## REST API

| Method and path | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| `GET /v1/tenants/{tenant}/pact-health` | Every pair's health over a `window` (`7d`, `30d` or `90d`), worst first. Narrow by `provider`, `consumer` and `state`. |
| `GET …/pact-health/providers/{provider}/consumers/{consumer}` | One pair's health and its trend, one bucket per day (per week for `90d`). |

Each signal carries a `status`, a reason `code` and a `summary`. An unknown `window` or `state`
answers `400` (`pact-health-window-invalid`, `pact-health-state-invalid`); a consumer with no pact
for the provider answers `404` (`pact-health-pair-not-found`). See the
[REST reference](/reference/rest/pact-broker).

Health covers Pact signals only. SLA breaches, consent and disputes on an agreement are added by the
contract agreements work, which builds on this rollup.
