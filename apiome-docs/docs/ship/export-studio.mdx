The **Export studio** converts one version — or one catalog item — into another format, and shows
you what the conversion keeps and loses **before** you generate it. Six steps run across the top:
**Source**, **Target**, **Options**, **Overlays**, **Verify** and **Review & Generate**. **Back** and
the step's main button at the foot move between them.

<Screenshot id="export-studio" alt="Export studio on the Target step: compact target tiles grouped by family, each with its three-segment fidelity meter" />

## Open the studio

The studio always exports a specific source, so open it from one:

- **A version** — in a version's **Export** panel (*Convert to any format*), **click “Export this
  version”**, a target chip, or **Re-run in Studio** on a recent export. In the **Export** dialog,
  **“Open in Export Studio”** carries your target and options across.
- **A project** — on **Versions**, **click “Export studio”** on the project's facts card, or choose
  **Export studio** in the header's **⋯** menu. Both open the studio on the project's head version.
- **A catalog item** — choose **Export to another format…** in the item's menu on the
  [Catalog](../bring-in/catalog.mdx), or in the **⋯** menu on the item's page. (The menu's **Export** row
  and the item page's **“Export”** button open the in-place **Export** dialog.) Exporting a catalog item produces a file; it
  never turns the item into a project.

Opened without a source — from **Export studio** in the rail or the command palette — the
**Source** step lets you choose one:

1. **Click “Catalog items”** and type in the search box to filter by name, slug, format or
   protocol. **Click an item** to export it.
2. Or **click “Projects”**, **click a project**, then **click a version** — or **“Latest version”**.
   Catalog items are not listed here: they stay catalog items until you convert them.

The studio then opens on that source at **2. Target**, and the address is a link you can share.

The trail above the title reads *your workspace › Ship › Export studio*. **“Back to Versions”** (or
**“Back to Catalog”**, when you came from the Catalog) sits beside **“Copy link”** at the top right
and returns you to where you opened the studio.

**“Copy link”** in the header copies a link that reopens the same source, target, options and step.
Credentials are never included in it; a link with a value that cannot be used opens with a yellow
notice saying what was ignored.

## 1. Source

A catalog item whose source file was not kept at import (or was not stored because it contained
credentials) cannot be exported. The studio says so and offers **"Re-import"**, which opens
**Import to catalog**; see [When an item has nothing to export from](./export-a-spec.md#when-an-item-has-nothing-to-export-from).

The source's name, its version and how many export targets are available. A catalog item also shows
its format and counts of services, operations, types and channels. **Click “Choose target”.**

<Screenshot id="export-studio-source" alt="Export studio on the Source step: Pet Store API, version 1.2.0, ten export targets" />

## 2. Target

**Choose a target format.** Cards are grouped — **REST & HTTP**, **API clients & collections**,
**RPC**, **Events**, **Data schema & graph**, **Agents & tools**, **Other targets**, **Healthcare** —
and each carries a **fidelity tier** computed for *this* source. **Healthcare** holds **FHIR** and
**HL7 v2**; the FHIR card's options pick **fhir_version** (`R4` or `R5`) and **packaging** (`bundle`
or `per-type`). **API clients & collections** holds the targets that
export saved requests rather than a contract: Postman, Hoppscotch, Thunder Client and the `.http`
request file (see [Export to an API client](./export-to-an-api-client.md)).

Each tile names the target, its family and, where the catalog knows it, the format's own colour
on the tile. Under the name, a three-segment **fidelity meter** and the tier say how much of this
source the target carries:

| Tier | Meter | Means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `lossless` | three green segments | Carries the source cleanly. |
| `lossy` | two amber segments | Drops or approximates some constructs. |
| `types-only` | one segment | Only the schemas survive; operations and channels are dropped. |

A readiness badge (**ready**, **check first**, **blocked**, **unavailable**) appears when the
pre-flight check has run; blocked and unavailable cards cannot be chosen and say why. Toggle
**Sorted by readiness** / **Sorted by name** above the grid. How tiers and readiness are worked out
is in [Understand export fidelity](./export-fidelity.md).

When the source was imported in another format, the studio hides the same-format target and offers
**Original source** instead: **“Download original”** gives you the file unchanged.

Select a card — the foot reads *Exporting to gRPC / Protobuf* with its tier and **% preserved** — and
**click “Continue”**.

### Several targets

To export the source to more than one format at once, **tick “Several targets”** above the cards.
The cards become a checklist, each with its tier for this source.

1. **Tick two or more targets.** **“Continue”** stays disabled until you have.
2. **Click “Continue”.** The studio skips **3. Options**: every target exports with its default
   options. On **4. Overlays**, add any overlays (they apply to every target), then **click
   “Continue”**.
3. On **5. Verify**, **click “Verify all targets”**. One dry run measures every target; a table lists
   each target's tier and **% preserved**, and the line above it names the lowest fidelity. Nothing
   is generated yet. **Click “Continue to review”.**

   <Screenshot id="export-studio-multi-verify" alt="Verify with several targets: OpenAPI 3.1 lossless, GraphQL SDL and Protocol Buffers lossy, lowest fidelity 64% preserved" />

4. On **6. Review & Generate**, **click “Generate”**, then **“Download .zip”**. The archive has a
   folder per target and a `manifest.json`.

Ticking or unticking a target clears the verification, so run it again. Untick **Several targets**
to go back to choosing one card.

### Gateway targets

**Kong Declarative Config**, **Gateway API HTTPRoute**, Envoy, Istio, Traefik, AWS API Gateway,
Azure API Management and Apigee write a gateway's own configuration and sit under **REST & HTTP**.
Most of them carry routes but no request or response schemas, so schemas are reported as dropped.
See [Export to an API gateway](./export-to-a-gateway.md) for what each one writes.

## 3. Options

The target's options: checkboxes, a row of choices, or text fields. Errors appear under the field,
for example *… is required.* A target with no options says so. **Click “Continue”.**

## 4. Overlays

Optional. An [OpenAPI Overlay](../bring-in/overlays.md) changes the exported document without
changing the version: a partner's servers, public descriptions, `x-` extensions. With no overlays,
the step says *No overlays — the version exports as it is.* **Click “Continue”.**

To add overlays:

1. **Click “Add overlay files”** and pick one or more overlay files (`.yaml`, `.yml` or `.json`).
   Each one is listed with its title and number of actions.
2. Put them in order. They apply from the top down, so the last one wins a conflict. Use the
   **up** and **down** arrows to move one, and **×** to remove it.
3. **Click “Continue”.**

<Screenshot id="export-studio-overlay" alt="Overlays step: two overlay files listed in the order they apply, with move and remove buttons" />

A file that is not an overlay, does not parse, or is larger than 1 MB is listed under the button
and not added. An export applies at most 10 overlays. Uploading a file with the same name again
replaces it and keeps its place.

Overlays apply only to versions imported from OpenAPI. For any other version, Verify reports
`OVERLAY_SOURCE_UNSUPPORTED`; remove the overlays to continue.

## 5. Verify

**Click “Run verification”** (or turn on **Verify automatically**). Fidelity, validation and lint run
in one pass; nothing is generated or stored yet.

<Screenshot id="export-verify" alt="Verify the gRPC / Protobuf conversion: Lossy — acknowledge to continue, 64% preserved, and the acknowledgement checkbox" />

The banner gives the verdict:

| Verdict | To continue |
| --- | --- |
| **Clean** | Nothing — **“Continue to review”** is enabled. |
| **Lossy — acknowledge to continue** | Tick *I understand this conversion is lossy and want to export anyway.* |
| **Severe — acknowledge to continue** | Type `export produces a types-only artifact` in the box. |
| **Invalid — export blocked** | Fix the source or choose another target; this export cannot be generated. |

The **Fidelity**, **Validation** and **Lint** tabs carry counts. **Fidelity** shows the % preserved
and what was dropped, approximated or synthesized; **Show per-construct report** opens the
projection map, where each construct links to its reason. Changing the target, an option or the
overlays clears the verdict and the acknowledgement — run it again.

With overlays, an **Overlays** panel under the tabs says how many values they changed. It lists any
action that could not be applied (for example `overlay.unmatched-target`, an action whose target
matches nothing), then one row per changed value: the value's path, the change (**Added**,
**Replaced**, **Appended** or **Removed**) and the overlay that made it. The verdict, the lint grade
and the fidelity all describe the overlaid document.

**Lint** lints the *emitted* file, not the source. Apiome reads the file back and runs the same lint
rules an import of that format runs, so a GraphQL export is checked by the GraphQL rules and a
Protobuf export by the Protobuf rules. The tab shows the score and grade, the findings grouped by
severity, and the rules that fired. Lint is advice: it never changes the verdict or blocks the
export. A format Apiome cannot read back (a Postman collection, for example) says it was not linted
and why.

At the bottom of **Fidelity**, **click “See what a round-trip changes”** to check the prediction
against reality: the [round-trip comparison](#check-a-round-trip) opens under the tabs and runs.

## 6. Review & Generate

The summary lists the **Source**, the **Target** (tier and % preserved) and the **Options** you
changed. **Click “Generate”.** With overlays, the
generated file has their changes, and the **Overlays** panel shows the job's own list of changes.
The round-trip check and the mock server test drive still use the version without overlays.

<Screenshot id="export-studio-job" alt="Generating Protocol Buffers: running at 62%, the Emit stage active, a warning and Cancel" />

The job moves through **Load source**, **Analyze fidelity**, **Emit**, **Validate** and **Package**,
and is **QUEUED**, **RUNNING**, **COMPLETED**, **FAILED** or **CANCELED**. **“Cancel”** stops it while
it runs; **“Generate again”** restarts a canceled one.

When it completes:

- **One file** — read it in the viewer, then **“Download *file-name*”** or **“Download .zip”**.
- **A bundle** — browse the files in the tree, then **“Download .zip”**.
- **Keep it** — **click “Save as preset”** to save the targets, options and delivery; apply a preset on
  **2. Target** with **Apply a preset** (see [Save an export as a preset](./export-presets.mdx)).
- **Deliver it** — tick delivery profiles under **Deliver when it completes** before you click
  **“Generate”**, or choose one under **Deliveries** and **click “Deliver now”**. The **Delivery** tab
  manages the profiles; see [Deliver an export](./deliver-an-export.mdx).
- **Artifact entities**, **Problems**, the mapping graph and **What … loses, worst first** explain
  the result. The **Fidelity overlay** paints the fidelity report onto the mapping graph (see
  [See where the export loses fidelity](#see-where-the-export-loses-fidelity)), and **“Run
  round-trip check”** reads the file back and compares it with the source (see
  [Check a round trip](#check-a-round-trip)).
- **Test drive** tries the file with the tools that fit its format (see [Test drive](#test-drive)).

The viewer, the file tree, the problem markers and the large-file limits are described on
[Read an exported file](./read-an-exported-file.mdx).

### See where the export loses fidelity

The mapping graph, **What became of each entity in *target***, wears the fidelity report as an
overlay. The **Fidelity overlay** row above the graph has one chip per loss kind, with the report's
own count:

| Chip | Means |
| --- | --- |
| **✕ DROP** | The construct is not in the exported file. |
| **≈ APPROX** | The construct is there, but represented imperfectly. |
| **✚ SYNTH** | Something was invented to satisfy the target, such as a protobuf field number. |
| **✓ OK** | The construct was carried over unchanged. |

Each entity in the graph carries a small pill with the glyph of its worst finding and how many
findings it has: a Pet.id field exported to Protocol Buffers shows **≈ 3** (two approximations and a
synthesized field number).

- **Hover or focus an entity** to read its findings below the graph: each one's kind, severity,
  message and how it lands in the target.
- **Click a kind chip** to switch that kind off; the entities without any finding of the kinds left
  on fade out, in the graph and in the table. **“Show all kinds”** turns them all back on. The chips
  always show the report's counts, whatever is filtered.
- **Click “Treemap”** for a compact view of a large API: one tile per construct, grouped by type,
  service or channel, sized by its number of findings and marked with the glyph of its worst one.
  **Click a tile** to select its entity; **“Graph”** goes back.

The line under the chips says how many findings there are. When a finding names a construct the
graph does not draw (yet, or at all), it says how many, and the treemap still shows them.

### Read a database export as an ERD

A [SQL DDL export](./export-a-spec.md#sql-ddl-from-any-source), and its DBML, Prisma, Liquibase and
Flyway outputs, is judged as tables rather than as text. Above the generated file, **click "ERD"** to see
it as an entity-relationship diagram; **click "DDL text"** (**DBML text**, **Prisma schema text** or
**Changelog text**) to go back.

<Screenshot id="export-erd" alt="An order, order line and address model exported to PostgreSQL as an ERD: five tables, the junction table order_lines and the child table order_tags marked SYNTH, and dashed foreign keys" />

- **Every table** shows its columns with their type in the export's dialect and **NOT NULL**. **PK**
  marks the primary key, **FK** a foreign key and **UQ** a unique key.
- **Every foreign key** is a line from the referencing table to the referenced one. The selected table's
  keys are labelled, for example `ship_to_id → address.id`.
- **What the export made up** is marked **✚**: a generated key column, the foreign key a nested object
  became, and the **junction** table a list of objects became or the **child table** a list of values
  became (with **✚ SYNTH** and a dashed border). Their lines are dashed too. These are the decisions the
  fidelity report lists as SYNTH.
- **The selection is shared.** Click a table or a column to select the type or field it came from: the
  entity tree follows, and when you go back to DDL or DBML text the viewer scrolls to its `CREATE TABLE` /
  `Table` or column line. Selecting something in the tree highlights it in the diagram and brings its table into view.

Drag to pan, scroll or use **+** and **−** to zoom, and **⛶** to fit the whole diagram. The ERD is drawn
from the tables Apiome wrote, so it is the same whichever output you picked.

### Test drive

Under the generated file, **Test drive** has a tab for each tool that can try this format. Which tabs
appear comes from the format itself, so each target shows its own (an overview of the tools and when to use
each is on [Test drive an export](./test-drive-an-export.mdx)):

| Tab | What it does | Targets |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Swagger UI** | Interactive docs for the file: every operation, its parameters and responses; **Try it out** while the mock server runs | OpenAPI |
| **ReDoc** | A read-only reference of the file, in three panes | OpenAPI |
| **GraphiQL** | Explore the schema (docs and autocomplete) and run queries that answer with mock data | GraphQL |
| **AsyncAPI viewer** | The file as AsyncAPI documentation: its operations by channel, their messages and payloads, and the schemas, with a sidebar to jump between them | AsyncAPI |
| **Mock server** | Serves the file as a live API you can send requests to; see [Mock try-out](./mock-try-out.mdx#test-drive-an-export) | OpenAPI |
| **Toolchain checks** (or the check's name, when there is one) | Runs the format's own checks, such as `buf lint`, on the file and shows their output; see [Toolchain checks](#toolchain-checks) | OpenAPI (`redocly lint`), AsyncAPI (`asyncapi validate`), Protobuf (`buf lint`, `buf build`), TypeSpec (`tsp compile`), Smithy (`smithy build`), Cap'n Proto (`capnp compile`), Thrift (`thrift compile`), API Blueprint (`drafter parse`) |

<Screenshot id="export-studio-test-drive" alt="Test drive with the redocly lint tab open, showing it is installed with a Run button, and the Mock server tab disabled because mocks are disabled on this server" />

A tool that needs something this server does not have is still shown, greyed out, with the reason
under the tabs, for example *Mock server is unavailable on this server: Export test-drive mocks are
disabled on this server.* Ask your administrator to install the tool or turn the feature on. A
format with no tools shows no **Test drive**.

#### Swagger UI and ReDoc

**Swagger UI** opens first for an OpenAPI file, with ReDoc beside it. Both show the file you just
generated, read straight from the Studio: nothing is uploaded again.

<Screenshot id="export-studio-openapi-test-drive" alt="Test drive with the Swagger UI tab open on Pet Store API 1.2.0: four pets operations, and the note that the sandbox makes no network requests" />

- **They make no network requests.** Each viewer runs in a sandbox with no access to your session or
  the rest of Apiome, and cannot reach the network.
- **Try it out needs the mock server.** On the **Mock server** tab, **click "Start mock"**. Swagger UI
  then points the file's servers at the mock, and **Try it out** → **Execute** sends the request to it
  through Apiome. It sends the method and path; a request body is not passed on. Without a running
  mock, **Try it out** is hidden. ReDoc never sends requests.
- **Large files open collapsed.** A file with more than 300 operations (or over 1 MB) opens with every
  section closed, so the page stays quick; open the tag you need.

#### GraphiQL

For a GraphQL file, **GraphiQL** opens on the schema you just generated, with an example query
already answered. Every answer is **mock data**: values of the right type that your browser makes
up from the schema. No server is involved, so you can try the schema before anything implements it.

<Screenshot id="export-studio-graphql-test-drive" alt="Test drive with the GraphiQL tab open on a pet store schema: an example pets query on the left and its mock data answer on the right, under the Mock data label" />

1. **Read the schema.** **Click the book icon** (**Show Documentation Explorer**) to browse every
   type, field and argument, with its description.
2. **Write a query.** Type in the editor; **Ctrl+Space** suggests the fields, arguments and enum
   values the schema allows, and mistakes are underlined as you type.
3. **Run it.** **Click the run button** (**Execute query**), or press **Ctrl+Enter**. The answer has the shape the schema
   promises: `ID`s like `id-1`, text as `Example text`, a real value of each enum, two items in each
   list, and custom scalars by their name (a `DateTime` gets a timestamp, a `UUID` a UUID). Ask the
   same question twice and you get the same answer.

- **It makes no network requests.** GraphiQL runs in a sandbox with no access to your session or
  the rest of Apiome, and cannot reach the network. The **Mock data** label above it, and in its
  corner, is a reminder that the answers are made up.
- **Queries and mutations are answered; subscriptions are not.** A subscription answers with
  *Subscriptions are not mocked in the test drive.*
- **A file GraphiQL cannot read shows a notice instead**, with the reason, for example
  `The file is not valid GraphQL SDL: Syntax Error: Expected Name, found <EOF>.` Directives the file
  uses without declaring them, such as federation's `@key`, do not stop it.

#### AsyncAPI viewer

For an AsyncAPI file, **AsyncAPI viewer** draws the file you just generated with the standard
AsyncAPI documentation renderer. It reads AsyncAPI 3.x and 2.x, so it shows the file whichever
AsyncAPI version you export (3.1, 3.0 or 2.6). The note above it counts the
file's channels and operations, for example *AsyncAPI 3.1.0 · 2 channels · 2 operations*.

<Screenshot id="export-studio-asyncapi-test-drive" alt="Test drive with the AsyncAPI viewer tab open on Order Events 1.2.0: the RECEIVE operation on orders/created with its OrderCreated message, and the menu button at the bottom right" />

1. **Read the operations.** Each operation shows its action (**SEND** or **RECEIVE**; **PUB** or
   **SUB** in a 2.x file), its channel address, and the message it carries with its payload.
2. **Jump to a channel.** **Click the menu button** (☰, bottom right) to open the sidebar, then
   **click an operation**. The sidebar lists every operation by channel, then the messages and
   schemas. When the window is wide enough, the sidebar stays open on the left.
3. **Look inside a payload.** **Click "Expand all"** on a payload to open every property, or
   **click "Payload"** under **Examples** for a generated example.

- **It makes no network requests.** The viewer runs in a sandbox with no access to your session or
  the rest of Apiome, and cannot reach the network.
- **A file the renderer rejects shows its errors**, line by line, in the viewer and in a notice above
  it, for example `There are errors in your Asyncapi document: "info" property must have required
  property "version"`. A file with no `asyncapi` version, or a version other than 2.x or 3.x, shows a
  notice instead of the viewer.

#### Toolchain checks

For formats with their own tools, such as Protocol Buffers, TypeSpec or Smithy, the real test of the
file is that tool. **Toolchain checks** lists the checks the format declares, such as `buf lint` and
`buf build`, and runs them on this server against the file you just generated.

<Screenshot id="export-studio-toolchain-console" alt="Test drive with the Toolchain checks tab open: buf lint failed with exit 100 and its console lists two naming findings in acme/v1/shop.proto; buf build passed. Above, the Toolchain checks summary repeats both results" />

1. **Click "Run"** beside a check. Its console opens under it and fills as the tool writes. Error
   output (stderr) is marked with a red bar beside it. The console's first line is the command, such
   as `$ buf lint`.
2. **Read the result.** When the tool finishes, a chip says how it ended:
   - **Passed**: the tool exited 0.
   - **Failed · exit 100**: the tool exited with that code (the file did not pass the check).
   - **Timed out**: the tool ran too long and was stopped.
   - **Not installed**: the tool was removed from this server after the page loaded.
   - **Could not run**: the check did not start; the reason is under the console.

   The time it took is shown at the top right of the console.
3. **Click "Copy"** to copy the console as text: the command, the output, and the result.
4. **Click "Run again"** after you change the source and generate again.

- **Results join the verify report.** Each finished check is listed under **Toolchain checks**, below
  the verify result at the top of **6. Review & Generate**. A new target, new options or a new
  **Generate** clears the list.
- **It checks the generated file.** The server runs the tool on the file of your last **Generate**.
  Nothing is uploaded from your browser.
- **The tool runs in a sandbox** with no network access, a time limit and a cap on output. Colour
  codes and other control characters in its output are removed, never applied to the page.
- **A tool that is not installed has no "Run" button.** Its row shows the reason, for example
  *The buf toolchain is not installed on this server.* Ask your administrator to install it.
- **Runs are rate limited.** Each person can run 10 checks a minute by default. Past that, the console
  says *Too many toolchain checks in a short time. Wait a moment, then run it again.* Administrators
  change the limit with `APIOME_EXPORT_TOOL_CHECK_RATE_LIMIT_PER_MINUTE`.

The same check runs from the API with
[`POST /v1/export/{tenant}/verify/tool-checks/{tool}`](../reference/rest/export.md#run-export-tool-check-v1-export-tenant-slug-verify-tool-checks-tool-post):
name the check in the body (`"check": "lint"`) with the same `artifact`, `version`, `target` and
`options` as `POST …/verify`, and a `jobId` to check a finished job's file. The answer streams one
JSON event per line: `start`, an `output` event per chunk, and a closing `result` with `status` and
`exit_code`.

The list also comes back from the API, as `test_tools` on each target of
[`GET /v1/export/{tenant}/targets`](../reference/rest/export.md#list-export-targets-v1-export-tenant-slug-targets-get). Each entry gives the tool's `kind`,
`label` and `params`, whether it runs on the server, and, for server tools, `available` and
`unavailable_reason`. The format's exporter declares its tools, so a new format brings its own tabs.

### Check a round trip

**“Run round-trip check”** (on **Review**, or **“See what a round-trip changes”** on the **Fidelity**
tab) exports the source, reads the file back into Apiome and compares the result with the source.
It runs only when you click it. The banner says whether every difference was expected:

- **Round trip verified**: the fidelity report explains every difference.
- **Fidelity report incomplete**: something changed that the report did not predict. **“Report a
  fidelity bug”** opens a pre-filled issue that carries only the reproduction details, never your
  API.
- **Comparison skipped**: Apiome cannot read this format back, so there is nothing to compare.

Two tabs show the differences:

- **Model diff** lists what changed in the API itself. **Predicted drops** lists every construct
  the fidelity report says the export drops, and whether the round trip saw it go. **Expected
  differences** pairs each change with the finding that explains it, and **Unexplained
  differences** lists the rest.
- **Text diff** shows the exported file beside the same file after it is read back and exported
  again, side by side, so you can see exactly what one more import and export changes. A file that
  comes back byte for byte says so. A multi-file bundle, a binary file or a very large one has no
  text diff; the tab says why, and **Model diff** still covers it.

<Screenshot id="export-roundtrip-model-diff" alt="Round-trip comparison for GraphQL SDL on Model diff: one of two predicted drops seen, two expected differences and one unexplained change to type Pet" />

#### Measured loss from the API

The same check is available as
[`POST /v1/export/{tenant_slug}/roundtrip`](../reference/rest/export.md#roundtrip-export-v1-export-tenant-slug-roundtrip-post).
Its `measurement` field compares the predicted fidelity report with what the round trip actually
did. It is `null` when the comparison was skipped or the file could not be read back.

| Field | What it holds |
| --- | --- |
| `empirical_losses` | Every difference between the source and the file read back, as a loss: removed → `drop`, added → `synth`, changed → `approx`. `predicted` says whether the fidelity report explains it. |
| `drops` | Every `drop` the report predicts. `corroborated` is true when the round trip removed or changed that construct (or the entity that holds it); `evidence` lists those differences. |
| `divergences` | Every place prediction and measurement disagree: `unpredicted_loss` (a difference the report did not predict), `unobserved_drop` (a predicted drop that did not happen) and `overclaim` (a construct the report calls preserved that changed). |
| `diverges` | `true` when `divergences` is not empty. |

`measurement` does not change `status`. An `unobserved_drop` means the report is more cautious than
it needs to be, so the round trip can still be **Round trip verified**.

### When generation fails

A failure card names the stage and offers the next step — for example *The emitted artifact failed
validation* with **“Review in Verify”**, or *Target not available* with **“Choose a different
target”**. **“Retry export”** is offered whenever the job can be retried.

## Who can export

Every member of a workspace can use the whole studio, whatever their role: **Viewer**, **Editor**
and **Owner** all see the same buttons, and the REST API answers them the same way.

| Action | Viewer | Editor | Owner |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Choose a target, preview and verify a conversion | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |
| **“Generate”**, for one target or several | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |
| **Export *n*…** from the Catalog's bulk bar | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |
| See **Recent export jobs** — everyone's, not only yours | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |
| Poll, download or cancel a job, including another member's | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |
| Test-drive an export on a mock server | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |

Jobs belong to the workspace, not to the person who started them. Nobody outside the workspace can
reach them, and a job is never found from another workspace — not even by someone who belongs to both.

The table is `docs/export-permission-matrix.json` in the repository; the role tests in apiome-rest
and apiome-ui read it, so the studio and the API cannot drift apart. See
[Roles](../workspace/roles.mdx) for everything else each role can do.

## With the keyboard

Every step works without a mouse. `Tab` and `Shift`+`Tab` move between controls; `↵` presses a
button (buttons also take `Space`); `Space` ticks a checkbox or picks a radio.

1. **Source** — opened without a source, `Tab` to the **Catalog items** tab and use `←` `→` to
   switch to **Projects**. `Tab` to the search box and type, then `Tab` to an item and press `↵`.
   For a project, press `↵` on it, then on a version or **Latest version**; **All projects** goes back.
   With a source, `Tab` to **“Choose target”** and press `↵`.
2. **Target** — `Tab` through the target cards and press `↵` on one; the card reads as pressed.
   For several targets, press `Space` on **Several targets**, then `Space` on each target.
   `Tab` to **“Continue”** and press `↵`.
3. **Options** — `Tab` through the fields and type; then `↵` on **“Continue”**.
4. **Verify** — press `↵` on **“Run verification”**. `←` `→` move between the result tabs. For a
   lossy conversion, `Tab` to **I understand this conversion is lossy and want to export anyway**
   and press `Space` (a types-only conversion asks you to type the phrase shown instead). Then `↵`
   on **“Continue to review”**.
5. **Review & Generate** — press `↵` on **“Generate”**, then on **“Download .zip”** or the file's
   **Download** button.

Each time the step changes, focus moves to the new step, which is read as *Step N of 5* and its name.
`↵` on **“Back”** returns to the step before.

In the Catalog's **Export *n*…** dialog, focus starts inside the dialog and `Tab` stays there.
`Space` picks the target; `↵` on **“Generate”**, then on **“Download .zip”**. `Esc` or **“Close”**
closes the dialog and puts focus back on **Export *n*…**.

Every step, the source picker, the target grid and the bulk dialog are checked by the
[accessibility gate](../workspace/accessibility.md) in the light, dark and high-contrast themes.
