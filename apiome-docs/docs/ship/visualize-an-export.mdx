A generated file is easiest to check as text when it is small. For anything larger, or a format judged
by its structure, the **6. Review & Generate** step of the [Export studio](./export-studio.mdx) also
draws it: what became of each part of your API, **where** the export loses fidelity, and the shape of
the file itself, as tables, files, services or channels.

<Screenshot id="export-fidelity-overlay" alt="The fidelity overlay on a Protocol Buffers export of the Petstore: every field marked with its findings, and the findings for Pet.id below the graph" />

## When to visualize

- **Before you ship a lossy export.** The fidelity report lists every finding; the graph and the
  treemap show which types, fields and operations they fall on, worst first.
- **When a reviewer reads diagrams, not text.** A database team reads tables and keys, an RPC team reads
  services and messages, an event team reads channels.
- **To see what the export made up.** Generated keys, junction tables, field numbers and other invented
  parts are marked in every picture.

## How the pieces fit

- **One selection everywhere.** Selecting a node in any picture, a cell of the treemap or a row of the
  entity tree selects the same part of your API: the tree, the graph and the file follow, and going back
  to the text scrolls to its line.
- **The fidelity counts are the report's.** The overlay's chips print the fidelity report's own counts,
  and the line under them says when a finding sits on something the graph does not draw.
- **Pictures are drawn from the file.** The ERD, the import graph, the service map and the channel
  topology come from the file Apiome wrote, so they show what the export holds, not what the source had.
- **The format decides the pictures.** Each export format declares which pictures it has; a format with
  none shows no switch. The picture you pick is kept for that format until you close the browser tab.
- **Made-up parts are marked, not hidden.** What the export invented carries **✚** (and **✚ SYNTH** on a
  table), the same mark the fidelity report uses.
