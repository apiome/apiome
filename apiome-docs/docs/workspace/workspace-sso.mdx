**Single sign-on** lets everyone at your company sign in to Apiome with their work account, through
your own identity provider (IdP) — Okta, Microsoft Entra ID, Google Workspace, Keycloak, Auth0 or
any other that speaks **SAML 2.0** or **OpenID Connect (OIDC)**. You set it up in three steps:
**connect** the IdP, **verify** an email domain you own, and **enable** the provider. **Go to
Workspace → Single sign-on.**

<Screenshot id="sso-providers" alt="Single sign-on: Northwind Okta (OIDC, northwind.io verified, 42 members, Live) and Northwind Entra ID (SAML 2.0, northwind.co.uk not verified, Disabled), with Add provider in the header" />

The page is for workspace **owners and admins**. Everyone else sees *Owners and admins only*.

Each provider in the list shows its protocol, its domains and how many are verified, how many
members have signed in through it, and one of three states:

| State | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **Live** | Enabled, with at least one verified domain. People at those domains are sent to it when they sign in |
| **Needs a verified domain** | Enabled, but no domain is verified yet, so nobody is sent to it |
| **Disabled** | Off. Nobody is sent to it |

## Connect your identity provider

Use whichever protocol your IdP offers — most offer both.

| | OIDC | SAML 2.0 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **You give Apiome** | The issuer URL, a client ID and a client secret | The IdP's metadata URL, or its metadata XML pasted in |
| **You give the IdP** | The **Redirect URI** Apiome shows | The **ACS URL** and the **SP entity ID** Apiome shows |
| **Where the email comes from** | The ID token's `email` — the IdP must mark it verified | The NameID, or an attribute you name |

1. **Click “Add provider”.**
2. Choose **OIDC** or **SAML 2.0**, and give it a **Name** — for example `Northwind Okta`. The **ID**
   fills in from the name; it appears in sign-in URLs and in the audit trail as `sso:<id>`.
3. For **OIDC**, enter the **Issuer URL** (for example `https://northwind.okta.com`), the **Client
   ID** and the **Client secret** from the app you registered at the IdP. Apiome reads the issuer's
   discovery document and checks it before saving.

   For **SAML 2.0**, enter the **IdP metadata URL** or paste the **IdP metadata XML**, then choose
   where the email address comes from: **The NameID**, or **An attribute** and its name (for example
   `mail`). Add an **SP signing key** only if your IdP requires signed sign-in requests.
4. Choose a **Default role** — the role a member gets the first time they sign in through this
   provider — or leave **Workspace default**. **Owner** is not offered.
5. **Click “Add provider”.** The provider is added **Disabled** and its drawer opens.

The client secret and the signing key are stored encrypted and never shown again.

Apiome refuses an IdP it could never sign anyone in through, and says why — for example a discovery
document that names a different issuer, or an IdP that offers single sign-on only over
HTTP-Artifact.

### Give the IdP its values

**Click a provider's name** to open its drawer. The **Configure** tab starts with what your IdP's
app settings need — the **Redirect URI** for OIDC, or the **ACS URL (reply URL)** and **SP entity ID
(audience)** for SAML — each with a **“Copy”** button.

<Screenshot id="sso-provider-configure" alt="The Northwind Entra ID drawer on Configure: ACS URL, SP entity ID and SP metadata with Copy buttons, then Name, IdP metadata URL and XML, Email address comes from, SP signing key and Default role" />

Below them you can change the provider's settings. Leave **Client secret**, **SP signing key** and
the two metadata fields blank to keep what is stored — Apiome never reads a secret back.
**Click “Save changes”** when you are done.

## Verify a domain

An IdP may only sign in addresses at domains you have proved you own. Until a domain is verified,
it sends no one anywhere.

1. In the provider's drawer, **click the “Domains” tab**.
2. Type your email domain in **Add a domain** — for example `northwind.io` — and **click “Add
   domain”**.
3. At your DNS provider, add the **TXT** record the row shows. **“Copy”** copies each value:

   | Field | Value |
   | --- | --- |
   | **Type** | `TXT` |
   | **Name** | `_apiome-challenge.northwind.io` |
   | **Value** | The token shown for the domain |

4. **Click “Verify”.** Apiome looks the record up and tells you what it found.

<Screenshot id="sso-domain-verify" alt="The Domains tab: northwind.co.uk, Not verified, with its TXT record name and value, a Verify button and a Could not check result showing the DNS answer" />

| Result | What it means | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Verified** | The record holds the token. The domain now routes sign-ins | Nothing — you can remove the DNS record |
| **Record not found** | There is no TXT record at that name yet | Check the record name, wait for DNS to update and try again |
| **Record has another value** | A TXT record is there, but it is not this token. The answer is shown | Replace it with the value shown |
| **Could not check** | The lookup itself failed — a timeout, or the DNS servers did not answer. The answer is shown | Nothing was changed. Try again in a few minutes |

A domain is never marked verified unless the lookup succeeded and found the token.

You can check a domain **three times an hour**. After that, Apiome tells you how long to wait.

Apiome refuses some domains outright:

- **Public email providers** — `gmail.com`, `outlook.com`, `yahoo.com` and the like. Add a domain
  your organization owns.
- **A domain another workspace has verified.** A domain signs in through one identity provider
  only. If you own the domain, ask whoever verified it to remove it.

Adding a domain without verifying it reserves nothing: someone else who can prove the domain may
still verify it first.

**Click “Remove”** beside a domain to take it off the provider. Sign-ins from it stop at once.

## Turn it on

**Click “Enable”** at the foot of the drawer. The provider becomes **Live** as soon as it is enabled
and has a verified domain.

From then on, when someone types a work email at one of your verified domains on the
[sign-in page](../getting-started/sign-in.mdx#sign-in-with-your-companys-single-sign-on), the
password field gives way to **“Continue with SSO”**, which takes them to your IdP.

**Click “Disable”** to stop it. The very next sign-in no longer goes to the IdP.

## Remove a provider

1. **Click “Remove…”** at the foot of the drawer. Apiome tells you how many members have signed in
   through the provider.
2. **Click “Remove” followed by the provider's name** to confirm, or **“Keep it”** to go back.

Removing a provider deletes its domains and its stored secrets. Members who signed in through it
keep their accounts, but can no longer sign in this way — make sure they have a password or another
sign-in method first.

## What is recorded

Every change on this page is written to the workspace's access audit, with who made it:

| Action | When |
| --- | --- |
| `sso.provider.created` | A provider is added |
| `sso.provider.updated` | Settings change, the provider is enabled or disabled, or a domain is added or removed |
| `sso.domain.verified` | A domain passes its DNS check |
| `sso.provider.deleted` | A provider is removed |

No secret is ever written to the audit. Sign-in attempts themselves are recorded separately, as
`sso:<id>`.

## When something is refused

| Code | What it means | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `sso-domain-public` | The domain is a public email provider | Add a domain your organization owns |
| `sso-domain-claimed` | Another workspace has verified this domain | Ask its owner to remove it there |
| `sso-domain-exists` | The provider already lists this domain | Nothing to add |
| `sso-domain-invalid` | That is not an email domain such as `acme.com` | Check the spelling; wildcards are not accepted |
| `sso-verify-rate-limited` | The domain was checked three times in the last hour | Wait as long as the message says |
| `sso-default-role-invalid` | The default role is **Owner**, or not a role of this workspace | Choose another role |
| `sso-enable-incomplete` | An OIDC provider has no client secret | Add the client secret on **Configure**, then enable |
| `sso-slug-taken` | Another provider already uses that **ID** | Choose a different ID |

What happens when someone signs in, and the codes a failed sign-in shows, are on the admin guide's
[Single sign-on](../admin/single-sign-on.mdx#what-happens-when-someone-signs-in) page.

## For operators

- Single sign-on needs `AUTH_CONFIG_ENC_KEY` and `INTERNAL_SERVICE_TOKEN` set, as described under
  [For operators](../admin/single-sign-on.mdx#for-operators) in the admin guide.
- `APIOME_SSO_PUBLIC_DOMAINS` on apiome-rest replaces the built-in list of public email providers
  with your own comma-separated list.
- Domain checks use the DNS resolvers of the machine apiome-rest runs on.
- The installation administrator can still connect a provider for any workspace, and mark a domain
  verified without a DNS record, from the admin console's
  [Single sign-on](../admin/single-sign-on.mdx) card.
- The same operations are available over the REST API:
  [`/v1/tenants/{tenant}/sso/providers`](../reference/rest/sso.md).
