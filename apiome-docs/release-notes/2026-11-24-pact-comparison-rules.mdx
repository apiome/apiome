Bi-directional contract testing compares a consumer's pact with the provider's OpenAPI document
instead of replaying it against a running provider. The engine that makes that comparison is
built, and its complete rule set is published in
[Comparison rules](/ship/pact-broker/comparison-rules).

{/* truncate */}

- **Every interaction is checked end to end.** The request's method and path are resolved to an
  operation; path, query and header parameters and the request body are validated against the
  provider's schemas; the expected status, headers and body are validated against the response.
- **Requests and responses are read differently.** A request must satisfy the schema as written.
  A response may use fewer properties than the provider returns, but never one the provider does
  not declare.
- **Failures are named and located.** Each of the 24 failure codes, such as
  `request.operation.unknown`, `response.status.undeclared` and `response.body.unknown-property`,
  comes with a JSON Pointer into the pact and into the OpenAPI document, through any `$ref`.
- **Matching rules are read, not ignored.** Rules of Pact 2, 3 and 4 are mapped to JSON Schema
  constraints (`type`, `regex`, `integer`, `equality` and more) and checked against the provider's
  schema. A rule that has no exact mapping, such as `date` or `eachKey`, is listed with the reason.
- **Three verdicts.** `pass`, `fail`, and `partial`: nothing contradicts the pact, but something
  could not be compared in full. A `partial` result is never reported as `pass`.
- **OpenAPI 3.0, 3.1 and 3.2**, and pacts written by pact-js, pact-jvm and pact-go.

**Not yet available:** running the comparison for you, and showing its verdict in the matrix and
can-i-deploy. There is no screen and no endpoint for it in this release.
